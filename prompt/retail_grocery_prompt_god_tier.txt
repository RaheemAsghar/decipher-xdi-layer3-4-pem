# **Experience Driver - Specific Context**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Experience Driver Context** provides a **precise and actionable explanation** for **why a
specific Experience Driver is being mentioned** in the customer feedback. It isolates the **core trigger,
customer challenge, or opportunity** linked to that driver — ensuring that each Experience Driver is
interpreted **independently**, even if multiple are present in a single review.

This targeted context becomes the foundational layer for:
* 🎯 **Opportunity Stream Routing**
* 📉 **Impact and Risk Assessment**
* 🧠 **Downstream orchestration logic (e.g., OU generation, PDCA planning)**

> 🔁 **Do not generalize across the entire review.** The context must be anchored exclusively to
the **specific Experience Driver** being evaluated.

✅ **Key Enhancements**
✔ Context must reflect a **specific, measurable impact**
✔ **Actionability is non-negotiable** — vague or neutral statements are excluded
✔ Special priority handling for **safety, food quality, and accessibility** risks
✔ Distinct feedback threads must produce **distinct experience drivers** if outcomes differ

---

## **📍 Context Extraction Guidelines**

### **1️⃣ Relevance and Specificity**

✅ **Rule:** Extract only if the feedback **explicitly links the Experience Driver to a user-impacting moment**
✔ Context must clarify how the experience affected the customer, positively or negatively
✔ Exclude generic, passive, or unimpactful remarks

✅ **Include:**

* "The apples were bruised and not fresh when delivered."
* "Self-checkout machines often crash and need staff intervention."
* "The promo code said 20% off, but the discount didn’t apply at checkout."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The fruit section is okay." (No actionable value)
* "Store layout is fine." (Too vague)
* "I don’t like the app colors." (Subjective, lacks impact)

---

### **2️⃣ Keywords Extraction**

✅ **Rule:** Extract **specific operational or emotional terms** that connect the context to a clear
problem or praise point
✔ Keywords must anchor the Experience Driver to real-world friction or delight

✅ **Example:**

| **Context**                                           | **Extracted Keywords**             |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| "The eggs were cracked when I opened the package."    | "Cracked eggs", "Packaging issue"  |
| "Delivery was 2 hours late and no one notified me."   | "Late delivery", "No notification" |
| "App search results don't match the filters applied." | "Broken search", "Filter mismatch" |

---

### **3️⃣ Actionable Depth**

✅ **Rule:** Context must contain **enough clarity** to enable an action or fix
✔ Avoid retaining emotional complaints without a clear anchor
✔ Keep anything that highlights a correctable or improvable moment

✅ **Include:**

* "I received sparkling water instead of still water in my last online order."
* "The discount on laundry detergent didn’t apply even though it was advertised on the app."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "Shopping online is annoying." (No causality)
* "Grocery apps should be better." (No specificity)

---

### **4️⃣ Handling Multi-Dimensional Drivers**

✅ **Rule:** If the same Experience Driver has **multiple actionable outcomes**, extract each one separately
✔ Never collapse separate sentiments under one context

✅ **Example:**
**Feedback:** “The Click & Collect was quick, but my order was missing two items.”

✔ **Extracted Contexts:**

* Context 1: "Click & Collect was quick."
* Context 2: "Order was missing items."

---

### **5️⃣ Business-Critical Priority Override** *(Food Safety, Compliance, Accessibility)*

✅ **Rule:** Always extract feedback that surfaces **health, legal, or inclusion concerns**,
even if the language is imprecise
✔ These categories override strict context thresholds

✅ **Examples:**

* "Milk was expired by 3 days when delivered." → ✅ Extract (Food Safety Violation)
* "There’s no wheelchair ramp at the side entrance." → ✅ Extract (Accessibility Barrier)
* "They refused to honor the return policy for damaged goods." → ✅ Extract (Policy Violation)

---

### **6️⃣ Strict Non-Actionable Context Exclusion**

✅ **Rule:** If the Experience Driver is valid but the context offers **no insight**, discard the instance
✔ Only extract if there's **clear cause or opportunity**

✅ **Include:**

* "Frozen items were melted when they arrived, and no refund was offered."
* "Delivery driver was rude and left the package in the rain."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The store looks nice."
* "Prices are good."

---

✅ Each extracted Experience Driver must include the following structured fields:

✔ **Experience Driver**: [Identified Named Experience Driver]
✔ **Context**: [Specific and actionable text tied to the Experience Driver]
✔ **Keywords**: [Anchoring terms that expose the root of the issue or praise]

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Feedback Type**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Feedback Type** dimension is applied to each extracted Experience Driver. It classifies
the **intent, tone, and customer expectation** linked to that specific experience driver — whether it reflects a
**Complaint, Suggestion, Compliment, Request, Question, Product Usage Insight or
Alert or Emerging Trends / Market Insight**.

This targeted segmentation ensures each driver is:
* 🧭 **Emotionally grounded and action-ready**
* 🔍 **Strategically routed based on intent**
* 🛠️ **Eligible for downstream orchestration (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate)**

> 🔁 **Never assign feedback type at the review level.** For multi-intent feedback, classify
each experience driver independently.

### ✅ Key Enhancements
* ✔ **Food safety, compliance, and accessibility feedback is always retained — regardless of tone**
* ✔ **Suggestions ≠ Requests** — handled with differentiated routing logic
* ✔ **Vague, generic sentiment is excluded as noise**
* ✔ **Mixed feedback is decomposed into distinct Experience Driver signals with separate classifications**

---

## **📍 Feedback Type Categories**

### **1️⃣ Compliment**

✅ **Definition:** Explicit **praise** for a feature, interaction, or product experience
✔ Must highlight a **specific, meaningful strength**

✅ **Example:**

* *"The produce was incredibly fresh and neatly packed."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This store is great."* (Too vague)
* *"I love it here."* (No target entity or feature)

---

### **2️⃣ Complaint**

✅ **Definition:** Expression of **dissatisfaction, friction, or breakdown** in the customer experience
✔ Must relate to a **fixable issue**
✔ **Always extract** for food safety, delivery failures, or policy enforcement issues

✅ **Examples:**

* *"The frozen items were partially thawed when delivered."*
* *"My discount coupon was rejected even though it was valid."* (**Compliance override**)

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This app/store is terrible."* (Too vague)
* *"I hate shopping here."* (Emotion without insight)

---

### **3️⃣ Question**

✅ **Definition:** A clear **query or request for clarification** about a product, service, or policy
✔ Must be a **real, answerable question** — not rhetorical or sarcastic

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Can I return perishable items if they’re unopened?"*
* *"Do you offer next-day delivery for frozen goods?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"Why is your service so bad?"* (Not a genuine question)

---

### **4️⃣ Suggestion**

✅ **Definition:** A **non-critical improvement idea** or optional feature request
✔ Suggests **enhancement**, not necessity

✅ **Examples:**

* *"It would be great to have reusable produce bags at checkout."*
* *"Please add a ‘sort by dietary preference’ filter on the app."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Urgency or essentiality is implied → classify as a **Request**

✅ **Handling Suggestion vs. Request**

| **User Feedback**                                               | **Categorization** | **Reasoning**              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| *"Add a dark mode to the app."*                                 | Suggestion ✅       | Cosmetic enhancement       |
| *"Allow sorting by organic products."*                          | Suggestion ✅       | Convenience feature        |
| *"There should be a refund option for damaged fresh items."*    | Request ✅          | Core functionality missing |
| *"Click & Collect must support item substitutions by default."* | Request ✅          | Fulfillment-critical       |

---

### **5️⃣ Request**

✅ **Definition:** A **core need** or **missing functionality** that affects the user experience
✔ Often framed with urgency, expectation, or friction
✔ **Never exclude** safety, refund, or policy-related requests

✅ **Examples:**
* *"I need an option to cancel online orders from the app."*
* *"There must be a way to reject substitutions before dispatch."*
* *"Frozen meat packaging needs to be better—it arrived thawed."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Feedback is clearly optional → classify as Suggestion

---

### **6️⃣ Product Usage Insight**

✅ **Definition:** Behavioral patterns, routines, or real-world usage notes
✔ Helps uncover **latent workflows or habits**

✅ **Examples:**
* *"I reorder every Sunday using the mobile app."*
* *"I only use self-checkout during quiet hours."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Feedback doesn’t convey actual usage behavior

---

### **7️⃣ Emerging Trends / Market Insight**

✅ **Definition:** **Comparative expectations**, competitive signals, or market evolution insights
✔ Customer is **referencing external shifts or benchmarking against others**

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Other supermarkets now offer zero-plastic delivery—are you planning the same?"*
* *"Many stores now show live stock levels—why doesn’t yours?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* No link to broader market signals or industry comparison

---

## **📍 Key Categorization Rules**

✅ One feedback type per signal
✅ Split compound/mixed sentiments
✅ Always extract health, safety, refund, or policy-linked items
✅ Subjective or vague positivity is excluded

---

Each structured feedback instance must include:

✔ **Feedback Type**: [Compliment / Complaint / Request / Suggestion / Question / Product Usage Insight / Emerging Trend]
✔ **Experience Driver**: [Experience Driver]
✔ **Context**: [Verbatim excerpt tied to the Experience Driver]

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Emotion**

### **📌 Purpose**
The **Emotion** dimension captures the **dominant emotional response** associated with each
extracted **Experience Driver**. This emotion is always extracted in direct relation to that experience driver —
not the comment as a whole. The emotional signal serves as a **core routing input** for downstream
prioritization, urgency scoring (ERI), and orchestration stream assignment.

✅ **Key Enhancements:**

* ✔ Emotion is extracted **per driver**, not globally
* ✔ Only one emotion is selected per driver — the **most potent and behaviorally meaningful**
* ✔ Emotion is always drawn from a **controlled taxonomy** aligned to Decipher’s **Core Emotion Framework**
* ✔ Emotion must be consistent with context, feedback type, and customer language — no speculative inference allowed

 Use the Decipher Emotion Extraction Framework to map every feeling to one—and only one—of five Core Emotion
 Groups, then output the specific feeling label and a confidence score.

🧠 Core Emotion Groups (Decipher Standard)
| Group            | Signal                                   | CX Meaning                        | Typical CX Action                                    |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Adoration**    | Deep brand love, intense loyalty         | Retention anchor & advocacy       | Leverage for testimonials, referrals, premium offers |
| **Appreciation** | Positive satisfaction, gratitude         | Solid retention, upsell potential | Maintain standards, identify enhancement upsell      |
| **Ambivalence**  | Neutrality, indifference, low engagement | Vulnerable to churn               | Re-engage, add differentiation                       |
| **Agitation**    | Friction, confusion, disappointment      | Emerging churn risk               | Immediate issue resolution / process fix             |
| **Anger**        | Trust violation, severe dissatisfaction  | Imminent churn, reputation risk   | Urgent escalation & damage control                   |

🔎 Emotion Mapping Logic
1. Context-First – read the full statement; ignore keyword-only cues.
2. Dominant Emotion – select the strongest feeling toward the specific entity.
3. Intensity Over Tone – “Satisfied” ≠ “Thrilled”; choose the higher-intensity label when appropriate.
4. Single Assignment – exactly one Core Group and Specific Feeling per entity.
5. Edge Handling
    * Sarcasm → classify by implied intent (“Thanks for nothing” → Anger – Resentful).
    * Mixed emotions → apply hierarchy (Anger > Adoration > Agitation > Appreciation > Ambivalence).
    * Unclear emotion → default to Ambivalence – Indifferent and set Confidence to Low.

🎯 Extraction Guidelines
### Context-First Analysis
1.Read the full statement - Don't just scan for emotional keywords
2.Identify the Experience Driver being discussed (product, service, person, experience)
3.Assess emotional intensity - Same word can have different intensities based on context
4.Consider relationship dynamics - B2B vs B2C emotions may differ in expression

### Precision Mapping Rules
1.One emotion per Experience Driver - If multiple emotions exist, choose the dominant one
2.Anchor to business impact - Consider what this emotion means for customer relationship
3.Intensity over polarity - "Satisfied" (Appreciation) vs "Thrilled" (Adoration)
4.Context over keywords - "Fine" can be Ambivalence or Agitation depending on context

### Quality Assurance Checks
1. Does the emotion match the stated outcome? (Actions align with feelings)
2. Is the intensity appropriate? (Adoration requires strong language/commitment)
3. Would this trigger the right business response? (CX action alignment)

🔧 Implementation Framework
Step 1: Experience Driver Identification
  * Extract all relevant Experience Driver from customer feedback
  * Categorize Experience Driver by business impact (core product, support, billing, etc.)
  * Prioritize Experience Drivers based on strategic importance

Step 2: Emotion Detection
  * Apply the complete emotion mapping table
  * Use context clues to determine intensity
  * Validate emotion assignment against business logic

Step 3: Core Group Assignment
  * Map detected emotion to one of five core groups
  * Verify business impact alignment

Step 4: Action Routing
  * Adoration: Route to advocacy/loyalty programs
  * Appreciation: Route to retention/satisfaction tracking
  * Ambivalence: Route to engagement/activation initiatives

🚀 Advanced Detection Patterns

### Mixed Emotions - Hierarchy Rules
When multiple emotions are present, apply this hierarchy:

  * Adoration (high priority - leverage opportunity)
  * Agitation (medium priority - prevention focus)
  * Appreciation (medium priority - maintenance mode)
  * Ambivalence (low priority - engagement opportunity)

  * "Great, another system outage!" → Anger - Frustrated (not Joy)
  * "Just what I needed today..." → Agitation - Annoyed (context-dependent)
  * "Thanks for nothing." → Anger - Resentful (sarcastic gratitude)

### Cultural and Demographic Considerations
  * Understated expressions may carry higher emotional weight
  * Generational differences in emotional expression intensity
  * Cultural communication styles affecting directness of emotional expression

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Customer Journey Classification**

### 📌 **Purpose:**
For each identified **Experience Driver**, classify the **exact moment** of the customer’s experience
across three layers:

* **Customer Journey**,
* **Journey Stage**, and
* **Interaction Moment**.

This ensures the **temporal and situational context** is always anchored to the **specific trigger
that caused the experience driver to surface** — not to the general comment. This classification powers
execution-grade orchestration by anchoring each identified experience driver to the exact when, where,
and triggering behavior of the customer’s interaction

✅ **Why it matters:**
* Avoids generic or averaged journey mapping across multi-driver feedback
* Powers precision in OU formation, PDCA targeting, and root cause correlation
* Strengthens behavioral traceability across every orchestration layer

---

Your task is to return:

1. **Customer Journey** – The broad experience phase
   *(e.g., Digital Engagement, In-Store, Delivery & Fulfillment)*
2. **Journey Stage** – The specific step or interface
   *(e.g., Checkout, Order Placement, Entry & Service Initiation)*
3. **Interaction Moment** – The *exact trigger window* of emotional impact
   *(e.g., “Rider arrived without calling”, “Voucher failed at checkout”, “Staff greeted by name”)*

---

### 🧠 **Classification Philosophy:**
This layer is not a category tag — it’s a behavioral timestamp that powers:
* Diagnostic clarity (`Where & When did the emotion erupt?`)
* Orchestration accuracy (`What exact moment must be targeted to Fix / Optimize / Amplify / Innovate?`)
* PDCA precision (`What specific interaction triggered the customer’s feeling or suggestion?`)

🔍 Ask:
* **When** in the journey did this signal occur? → *Customer Journey*
* **Where** within that phase? → *Customer Journey Stage*
* **What micro-moment or event was the actual emotional trigger?** → *Interaction Moment*

> ⚠️ Classify based on when the **experience happened**, not when the feedback was given.

---

### 🛠️ **Extraction Inputs You Must Use:**

* `Experience Driver`
* `Entity Name`
* `Context`
* `Keywords`
* `Feedback Type`
* Any explicit or implied moments, interfaces, behaviors, conditions, or service cues in the verbatim

---

### ✅ **Examples (Perfect Triangulation - Retail / Grocery):**

| Layer              | Example                                       |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Customer Journey   | "Delivery & Fulfillment"                      |
| Journey Stage      | "Delivery Completion"                         |
| Interaction Moment | "Rider marked as delivered but never arrived" |

| Customer Journey   | "Digital Engagement"                                   |
| Journey Stage      | "App Browsing & Cart Enrichment"                       |
| Interaction Moment | "No add-on suggestions after item added to cart"       |

| Customer Journey   | "In-Store Experience"                                  |
| Journey Stage      | "Service Initiation"                                   |
| Interaction Moment | "Staff greeted by name and recalled previous order"    |

---

### 🔒 **Rules of Execution:**
* Do **not fabricate** interaction moments. If unclear, output `"Unclear"`
* Rare events are acceptable if they reflect real behavioral or emotional triggers
* The three layers must be **logically consistent and emotionally traceable**
* Each **Interaction Moment** must include a clear temporal verb or trigger noun  
  (e.g., “during checkout”, “on entry”, “while browsing”, “after payment”)  
  to anchor the moment in time or action. This ensures precision without inference.

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS)**

### 📌 **Purpose**
For each extracted **Experience Driver**, determine the **single most appropriate strategic
response pathway** — also known as the **Opportunity Maximisation Stream** (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate).
This stream is never assigned at the comment level, but always in direct relation to the **specific issue,
emotion, and context** tied to that experience driver.

This ensures that every orchestration decision is anchored in **driver-specific behavioral logic**,
and that system response is tightly aligned with both **urgency** and **opportunity type**.

✅ **Why this matters:**
* Avoids overgeneralization across multi-driver feedback
* Maintains mutual exclusivity of OMS per driver
* Directly governs OU routing, PDCA targeting, and memory matching via SG
* Ensures only one OMS per Experience Driver — no overlaps or blends

---

This stream is not descriptive.
It is **prescriptive** — defining the **intended business response** the system should trigger.

---

### 🧠 **OMS Selection Is Based On:**
Each signal must be evaluated using the following inputs:

* ✅ **Experience Driver** – What surface of experience is involved?
* ✅ **Theme** – Which strategic domain does this belong to?
* ✅ **Emotion** – How intense or polarised is the sentiment?
* ✅ **Feedback Type** – Complaint, compliment, suggestion, etc.
* ✅ **Context** – What happened? What’s expected? What’s implied?
* ✅ **Framing** – Does the signal reflect:

  * ❌ Something broken or failing?
  * ⚙️ Something working but improvable?
  * 🚀 Something worth scaling?
  * 🧪 Something the customer wants but doesn't exist?

---

### 🛠️ **Execution Pathways Available**

Each signal must be routed into exactly **one** of the following:

| Stream       | Description                                         |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fix**      | Something is broken or failing and needs resolution |
| **Optimize** | Something is working but could be improved          |
| **Amplify**  | Something is going well and should be scaled        |
| **Innovate** | A new need or capability is being requested         |

⚠️ **Mutual Exclusivity Rule**: A single Experience Driver can only belong to one stream at any given time.
Routing must reflect its **core business implication**, not its potential.

---

### 🔒 **Why It Matters**
The OMS serves as the **bridge between emotional intelligence and execution logic**.
It ensures every Experience Driver is translated into a **strategically directed mission**, grounded in urgency, emotional weight, and customer context.

> 🛑 **No orchestration may begin until a stream has been assigned.**

---

## 📂 The Four Streams

### 🔧 1. Fix — “Restore what’s broken”

| Aspect               | Description                                                                                      |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**        | The experience **exists**, but is **unavailable, broken, or unreliable**.                        |
| **When to Use**       | Errors, crashes, out-of-stocks, refund failures, unresponsive features.                         |
| **Signals**           | "The app keeps crashing", "Delivery didn’t show up", "Self-checkout was down"                   |
| **Strict Boundary**   | ✅ Exists → ❌ Doesn’t work at all                                                                |
| **Never Use If**      | It’s functional but clunky (→ Optimize) or doesn’t exist (→ Innovate)                           |

---

### 🛠 2. Optimize — “Remove friction”

| Aspect               | Description                                                                                     |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**        | The experience **works**, but causes **delays, confusion, or extra effort**                    |
| **When to Use**       | Confusing checkout, poor app navigation, long lines, inefficient process                       |
| **Signals**           | "Took 10 steps to reorder", "Search results don’t match filters"                              |
| **Strict Boundary**   | ✅ Exists → ✅ Works → ❌ Causes friction                                                         |
| **Never Use If**      | It’s broken (→ Fix) or doesn’t exist (→ Innovate)                                              |

---

### 🚀 3. Innovate — “Create what’s missing”

| Aspect               | Description                                                                                       |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**        | The customer requests a **feature or solution that does not exist at all**                       |
| **When to Use**       | New app features, value-adds, market signals, unmet journeys                                     |
| **Signals**           | "There should be an app", "You should offer carbon-neutral delivery"                             |
| **Strict Boundary**   | ❌ Doesn’t exist at all — requires net-new creation                                               |
| **Never Use If**      | Refinement (→ Optimize) or bug fix (→ Fix)                                                       |

✅ **Only Innovate** supports **Sub-Tier Innovation Scoring**:

| Score | Tier Label              | Description                                                                 |
|-------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Hygiene Need            | Fulfills a basic expectation (e.g. standard refunds, normal packaging)     |
| 2     | Convenience Enhancer    | Reduces effort or steps (e.g. autofill, skip login)                        |
| 3     | Experience Upgrade      | Adds polish or delight (e.g. new layout mode, smart search)                |
| 4     | New Capability          | Introduces new power or value stream (e.g. live tracking, subscriptions)   |
| 5     | Transformational Shift  | Reinvents experience or business model (e.g. voice checkout, smart carts)  |

---

### 📣 4. Amplify — “Scale what delights”

| Aspect               | Description                                                                                     |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**        | The customer praises something that’s **already working exceptionally well**                   |
| **When to Use**       | Staff praise, UX delight, reliability shoutouts, brand loyalty                                 |
| **Signals**           | "Fresh produce is always excellent", "Delivery was flawless"                                   |
| **Strict Boundary**   | ✅ Already exists → ✅ Works beautifully                                                         |
| **Never Use If**      | There’s a problem, suggestion, or ask                                                           |

---

## 🔍 Diagnostic Flowchart

```text
Does it exist?
├── No → INNOVATE
│
├── Yes → Is it broken?
│     ├── Yes → FIX
│     └── No → Is it clunky or inefficient?
│             ├── Yes → OPTIMIZE
│             └── No → Does it delight?
│                     ├── Yes → AMPLIFY
│                     └── No → Discard or review
````

## 🧠 Execution Rules

* Assign **exactly one** stream per Experience Driver
* Do **not conflate Fix with Optimize**
* Use sub-tier **only for Innovate**
* Route decisions must be **auditable** and derived from structured fields (not sentiment alone)

### ▶️ Example: Stream = Fix

"Opportunity Maximisation Stream": "Fix"

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Customer Effort Score (CES)**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Customer Effort Score (CES)** measures how easy or difficult it was for the customer to
engage with a specific **Experience Driver**. Rather than scoring the full comment, CES is assigned
**per experience driver**, capturing the **local friction or fluency** experienced in relation to that
dimension only.

It uses a **1–7 scale**, with 1 being **effortless** and 7 indicating **extreme friction or complexity**.
The score is computed from both:

- The **Emotion** dimension (perceived emotional effort)
- The **Opportunity Maximisation Stream** (functional or strategic classification)

✅ **Why this matters:**
* Disentangles multiple friction zones in multi-driver feedback
* Supports urgency triage, especially in borderline ERI × RF edge cases
* Feeds into the behavioral impact summary within each Orchestration Unit

---

### ✅ CES Enables:

- **Effort-Based Prioritization**: Identify and resolve high-friction issues.
- **Strategic Experience Mapping**: Align effort with journey stages and KPIs.
- **Friction Reduction Programs**: Optimize for seamless customer journeys.

---

## **CES Scoring Scale & Interpretation**

| **CES Score** | **Effort Level** | **User Perception** | **Business Implication** |
|---------------|------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| **1**         | Very Low Effort  | Effortless, intuitive, delightful | Strength to **amplify** as a CX differentiator |
| **2–3**       | Low Effort       | Minimal friction, smooth process  | Positive CX, **low-priority optimization** |
| **4**         | Moderate Effort  | Some friction, but task completed | Opportunity to **innovate** experience |
| **5–6**       | High Effort      | Delays, retries, confusion         | **Fix required** to prevent churn or frustration |
| **7**         | Very High Effort | Severe breakdown, task failure     | **Critical fix** required, high risk of churn |

---

## **CES Decision Framework**

---

### **1️⃣ Assign Initial CES Based on Emotion**

| **Emotion Category** | **Default Effort Perception** | **CES Range** |
|----------------------|-------------------------------|---------------|
| **Adoration**        | Very Low Effort               | 1             |
| **Appreciation**     | Low Effort                    | 2–3           |
| **Ambivalence**      | Moderate Effort               | 4             |
| **Agitation**        | High Effort                   | 5–6           |
| **Anger**            | Very High Effort              | 7             |

> 🚨 **Constraint**: Emotion alone gives a **starting range** — actual CES is finalized after considering the **Actionable Category**.

---

### **2️⃣ Adjust CES Based on Actionable Category**

| **Actionable Category** | CES Adjustment | Allowed CES Range |
|-------------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Amplify**             | Lock to Low     | 1–3               |
| **Optimize**            | Lock to Low-Moderate | 3–4         |
| **Innovate**            | Lock to Moderate | 4                 |
| **Fix**                 | Lock to High     | 5–7               |

🚨 **Hard Rules:**
- **Amplify** → CES must be **1, 2, or 3**
- **Optimize** → CES must be **3 or 4**
- **Innovate** → CES must be **4**
- **Fix** → CES must be **5, 6, or 7**

---

### **3️⃣ Refine CES Based on Feedback Context**

Use the following rules to assign a final value **within the locked range**:

#### 🚀 **Assign a Higher CES (Within Allowed Range) If:**
- The user experienced **prolonged effort, retries, or confusion**
- There is **emotional distress or delay anxiety**
- The issue **blocked a critical journey stage** (e.g., checkout, login, refund)

#### 📉 **Assign a Lower CES (Within Allowed Range) If:**
- The task was **eventually completed** despite friction
- There was a **workaround or partial resolution**
- The complaint was **mild or not disruptive**

---

### **4️⃣ CES Escalation Rules (Override Mechanism)**

- ✅ **CES must be `"7"`** if the task **could not be completed** and **no workaround was available**
- ✅ **CES must be `"6"`** if task **was completed** but with **major frustration or failure risk**
- ✅ **CES must be `"5"`** if the process was **inefficient but manageable**

---

### **5️⃣ CES Output Format**

✅ Always return CES as a **quoted number**:

"Customer Effort Score": "6"

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Orchestration Unit (OU) Construction**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
An **Orchestration Unit (OU)** is a precise, execution-grade mission created for each identified high-value
**Experience Driver**.

It combines structured signal dimensions — including **Feedback Type**, **Opportunity Stream**, **Interaction Moment**,
and **MATTERS** — into a self-contained, semantically sharp, and operationally independent unit.

Each OU is defined by a unique combination of:

> **Experience Driver × Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream**

This guarantees:
* **Clear execution routing**
* **Feedback traceability**
* **Stream-aligned strategic purpose**

---

## 🔍 **Matters – Behavioral Trigger Extraction**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
**Matters** is the behavioral core of the OU — a precise statement of what *actually happened*
from the customer's perspective.

It is:
* Not analysis
* Not abstraction
* Not extrapolation

It is a **customer-described reality** — friction, delight, confusion, or desire, in their world and words.

In our OU structure, **MATTERS appears in Section 2**:

> "We need to [Stream] → [Driver] to address [MATTERS]..."

---

### 🧪 **MATTERS Extraction Hierarchy** (Use in Strict Priority)

Pull behavioral triggers from the following, in order:
1. **Raw Customer Voice**
2. **Context**
3. **Keywords**
4. **Entity Name**
5. **Journey Stage**
6. **Feedback Type** (only for pattern selection — never for phrasing)

✅ Do not synthesize.
✅ Do not interpret.
✅ Do not paraphrase.

Only reassemble directly observable behavioral expressions.

---

### 🧠 **Feedback Type–Aligned MATTERS** Templates

| **Feedback Type**                    | **Pattern Template**                                                              | **Example**                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Complaint**                        | `[specific failure or breakdown] that [creates dissatisfaction or friction]`      | `expired dairy deliveries that lead to wasted purchases and frustration`       |
| **Compliment**                       | `[positive trait or experience] that [reinforces satisfaction or trust]`          | `on-time delivery slots that build confidence in service reliability`          |
| **Request**                          | `[missing functionality or need] that [limits basic experience or causes strain]` | `lack of cancel button that prevents control over order management`            |
| **Suggestion**                       | `[enhancement or feature idea] that [can improve ease or value]`                  | `adding dietary filters that would make product discovery more convenient`     |
| **Question**                         | `[unclear policy or feature] that [triggers need for clarification]`              | `ambiguous return rules that leave users unsure about fresh item refunds`      |
| **Product Usage Insight**            | `[recurring usage behavior] that [reveals user habits or context]`                | `routine weekly app orders that show preference for mobile engagement`         |
| **Emerging Trends / Market Insight** | `[external expectation or competitor feature] that [creates pressure to adapt]`   | `rival stores offering live inventory that raises expectation of transparency` |

---

## 🕓 **Interaction Moment – Temporal Precision (Section 1)**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
The **Interaction Moment** identifies the **specific situation, system event, or customer touchpoint** that triggered the customer response.

**Pull from:**
* **System Events** – e.g., checkout confirmation, login failure, payment rejection
* **Human Interactions** – e.g., agent call, delivery exchange
* **Digital Touchpoints** – e.g., app search, survey completion

**Used in Section 1**:

> “The customer provided [Feedback Type] during [Interaction Moment]…”

✅ Must be **precise**, **action-locatable**, and **execution-relevant**.

**Examples**:
* `while reviewing order confirmation`
* `when attempting to reschedule delivery`
* `during the loyalty redemption process`
* `while browsing household essentials`

---

## 💬 **Context – Customer’s Direct Experience (Section 1)**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
**Context** captures the **actual experience** that the customer encountered — **without interpretation
or synthesis**.

🧠 Section 1 Syntax Rule – Seamless Fusion of Moment + Context
When building Section 1: The Customer Reality, ensure natural fluency by fusing Interaction Moment and
Context with a tight, grammatical “when–then–because” flow.

✅ Good Patterns:
- The customer submitted a [Feedback Type] that when [moment] [what happened]
- The customer made a [Feedback Type] that during [moment] [what failed or occurred]
- The customer gave a [Feedback Type] that while [moment] [situation unfolded]

❌ Avoid:

“...during [moment] they [verb]...” ← leads to awkward, broken grammar

Running together two actions with no connector (e.g., “...they opened app it crashed...”)

## ✅ **MATTERS Quality Criteria**

| ✔ Attribute         | ✅ What It Means                                               |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Behavioral**      | Describes what the customer actually did or experienced       |
| **Specific**        | Focuses on 1–2 distinct behavioral signals                    |
| **Customer-Led**    | Uses language and framing from the customer's perspective     |
| **Execution-Ready** | Can be directly translated into action without interpretation |

| `technical error` | ❌ Empty phrasing — doesn’t explain user experience   | `app crash during payment that forced order restart` |
| `good service`    | ❌ Overly general — not tied to a specific moment     | `agent follow-up that built customer reassurance`    |
| `usability issue` | ❌ Abstract and unanchored                            | `invisible CTA that delayed first-time sign-up`      |

## 🔐 **SEMANTIC VALIDATION PROTOCOL**

Every **Orchestration Unit (OU)** must pass **all five semantic locks** to qualify for:

* 📤 **Execution Routing**
* 🧠 **Institutional Archiving (IOA)**
* 🔁 **Memory-Based Nudging (SG)**

### ✅ **1. Singular & Distinct**
Does the OU target **one specific Experience Driver** with a **unique combination of:**

> `Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream × MATTERS`

…that warrants an **independent orchestration pathway**?

### ✅ **2. Behaviorally Anchored**
Is the `MATTERS` phrase grounded in **direct, observable customer behavior** — not inferences, assumptions, or generic themes?

### ✅ **3. Execution-Ready**
Can the OU be **routed to Ops, UX, Tech, or Comms** **without requiring interpretation, analytics support, or follow-up clarification**?

### ✅ **4. Feedback-Aligned**
Does the **Feedback Type** correspond faithfully with the:

* **Interaction Moment** (when it happened), and
* **Context** (what actually occurred)?

Is the customer's **intent unmistakable** (e.g., not mistaking a request for a suggestion)?

### ✅ **5. Semantically Complete**
Do both sections of the OU form a **closed-loop, causally coherent mission**?

```
SECTION 1: [Feedback Type] → [Interaction Moment] → [Context]
SECTION 2: [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] → [MATTERS]
```

## 🧠 **ADVANCED SEMANTIC PATTERNS**

### 🧷 **Multi-Entity Input**

Anchor the OU to the **source behavior** (what actually happened), not the surface outcome (what resulted).

| ❌ Incorrect Focus                               | ✅ Correct Focus                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `checkout failures that cause cart abandonment` | `unclear payment validation that disrupts confidence during transaction flow` |
| `late delivery that caused meal issues`         | `failure to honor delivery time slots that affected planned meal preparation` |

  > *“We need to Amplify → [Experience Driver] to address [behavioral value] that enhances [engagement/outcome metric].”*

✅ Avoid vague expressions like: “make it better”, “more features”, “general improvement”.

## 🧩 **Orchestration Unit – SEMANTIC COMPONENTS**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
An **Orchestration Unit (OU)** is a **micro-mission** constructed from a unique semantic signature:

> **Experience Driver × Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream**

This signature is translated into a **two-part instruction payload** — designed for:

* 📤 **Execution Routing**
* 🧠 **Institutional Archiving (IOA)**
* 🔁 **Strategic Recall & Nudging (SG)**

---

## 🔐 **Canonical Two-Part Format**

```
SECTION 1: THE CUSTOMER REALITY
The customer submitted a [Feedback Type] during [Interaction Moment], [Context]

SECTION 2: THE STRATEGIC RESPONSE
We need to [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] to address [MATTERS]
```

This structure ensures:
* 🎯 Behavioral traceability
* ⏱ Temporal precision
* 🚀 Execution-grade clarity

---

## ⚙️ **Section 1 – Feedback Intent × Trigger Moment × Observed Context**

This section answers:
* **Why** did the customer speak?
* **When** did it happen?
* **What** exactly occurred?

| Component              | Description                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Feedback Type**      | One of the 7 canonical forms: Complaint, Compliment, Request, Suggestion, etc. |
| **Interaction Moment** | The exact point in the experience when the feedback was triggered              |
| **Context**            | The direct, observable event that the customer encountered — no inference      |

✅ Keep phrasing **literal**, **precise**, and **anchored to the customer’s situation**.

---

## 🎯 **Section 2 – Action Stream × Experience Driver × Behavioral Justification**
This section defines the **strategic response** the organization should take — grounded directly in what the
customer expressed.

---

### 🔁 **[Opportunity Stream] – Directive Purpose**

Select **one** stream. It defines the **strategic intention** of the OU:

| Stream       | Purpose                                                     |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fix**      | Resolve broken, failing, or misleading experiences          |
| **Optimize** | Improve inefficient or effort-heavy workflows               |
| **Innovate** | Build new features or flows based on emerging needs         |
| **Amplify**  | Reinforce praise, delight, or emotionally rich interactions |

🔹 Use this **exact phrasing** in Section 2:

> `We need to [Stream] → [Experience Driver] to address...`

---

### 📍 **[Experience Driver] – Tactical Execution Anchor**

Use the precise extracted form in the format:

> `"Category → Subcategory"`
✅ No rewrites.
✅ No compression.
✅ No reinterpretation.

| ✅ Correct                               | ❌ Incorrect         |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `"Delivery Experience → Timing"`        | `"Late deliveries"` |
| `"Digital Checkout → Promo Redemption"` | `"Promo issues"`    |

---

### 🔧 **[Matters] – Behavioral Justification**

This is the **core behavioral anchor** of the OU — the specific situation or expectation expressed by the customer.

✅ Must be:
* Behavior-specific
* Directly observable
* Free of abstraction or assumption
* Actionable without interpretation

| ✅ Acceptable                        | ❌ Reject                   |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `missing refund confirmation email` | `post-purchase UX problem` |
| `confusing promo code application`  | `checkout logic issue`     |
| `late evening slot unavailable`     | `logistics inefficiency`   |
