Each extraction must also include:

* A corresponding **Theme** (strategic or operational context)

downstream processing.

* ❌ Aliases or synonyms

# **Experience Driver - Specific Context**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Experience Driver Context** provides a **precise and actionable explanation** for **why a  
specific Experience Driver is being mentioned** in the customer feedback. It isolates the **core trigger,  
customer challenge, or opportunity** linked to that driver — ensuring that each Experience Driver is  
interpreted **independently**, even if multiple are present in a single review.

This targeted context becomes the foundational layer for:
* 🎯 **Opportunity Stream Routing**
* 📉 **Impact and Risk Assessment**
* 🧠 **Downstream orchestration logic (e.g., OU generation, PDCA planning)**

> 🔁 **Do not generalize across the entire review.** The context must be anchored exclusively to  
the **specific Experience Driver** being evaluated.

✅ **Key Enhancements**
✔ Context must reflect a **specific, measurable impact**  
✔ **Actionability is non-negotiable** — vague or neutral statements are excluded  
✔ Special priority handling for **fraud, compliance, and access-related risk**  
✔ Distinct feedback threads must produce **distinct experience drivers** if outcomes differ

---

## **📍 Context Extraction Guidelines**

### **1️⃣ Relevance and Specificity**

✅ **Rule:** Extract only if the feedback **explicitly links the Experience Driver to a user-impacting moment**  
✔ Context must clarify how the experience affected the customer, positively or negatively  
✔ Exclude generic, passive, or unimpactful remarks

✅ **Include:**

* "KYC failed even after I uploaded all the correct documents."  
* "Auto-debit ran twice and took extra money from my account."  
* "OTP arrives late, and I can’t log in when I need to transfer urgently."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The dashboard is okay." (No actionable value)  
* "I like the design." (Too vague)  
* "Finance apps are boring." (Subjective, lacks impact)

---

### **2️⃣ Keywords Extraction**

✅ **Rule:** Extract **specific operational or emotional terms** that connect the context to a clear  
problem or praise point  
✔ Keywords must anchor the Experience Driver to real-world friction or delight

✅ **Example:**

| **Context**                                               | **Extracted Keywords**                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| "My Aadhaar was rejected even though the photo was clear."| "KYC rejection", "Document verification error" |
| "The refund hasn't come after 10 days."                   | "Delayed refund", "Reversal delay"          |
| "The app froze when I tapped on transaction history."     | "App crash", "Transaction view failure"     |

---

### **3️⃣ Actionable Depth**

✅ **Rule:** Context must contain **enough clarity** to enable an action or fix  
✔ Avoid retaining emotional complaints without a clear anchor  
✔ Keep anything that highlights a correctable or improvable moment

✅ **Include:**

* "I was asked to re-upload my PAN three times before it was accepted."  
* "Support didn’t respond when I flagged a fraudulent debit."  

❌ **Exclude:**

* "Banking apps are annoying." (No causality)  
* "This app is not for me." (No specificity)

---

### **4️⃣ Handling Multi-Dimensional Drivers**

✅ **Rule:** If the same Experience Driver has **multiple actionable outcomes**, extract each one separately  
✔ Never collapse separate sentiments under one context

✅ **Example:**  
**Feedback:** “The UPI transfer was instant, but the amount was deducted twice.”

✔ **Extracted Contexts:**

* Context 1: "UPI transfer was instant."  
* Context 2: "Amount was deducted twice."

---

### **5️⃣ Business-Critical Priority Override** *(Fraud, Compliance, Access Barriers)*

✅ **Rule:** Always extract feedback that surfaces **financial, legal, or inclusion concerns**,  
even if the language is imprecise  
✔ These categories override strict context thresholds

✅ **Examples:**

* "Someone posed as your agent and scammed me on WhatsApp." → ✅ Extract (Fraud Risk)  
* "I can’t read the app properly — no dark mode for vision issues." → ✅ Extract (Accessibility)  
* "The app shared my bank info with other apps without asking." → ✅ Extract (Data Sharing Violation)

---

### **6️⃣ Strict Non-Actionable Context Exclusion**

✅ **Rule:** If the Experience Driver is valid but the context offers **no insight**, discard the instance  
✔ Only extract if there's **clear cause or opportunity**

✅ **Include:**

* "The EMI schedule didn’t update after I paid early."  
* "The app logged me out during a payment and I lost the transaction."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The app is decent."  
* "No complaints so far."

---

✔ **Context**: [Specific and actionable text tied to the Experience Driver]  
✔ **Keywords**: [Anchoring terms that expose the root of the issue or praise]

### **1️⃣ Insufficient Context**

✅ **Include:**

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Feedback Type**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Feedback Type** dimension is applied to each extracted Experience Driver. It classifies  
the **intent, tone, and customer expectation** linked to that specific experience driver — whether it reflects a  
**Complaint, Suggestion, Compliment, Request, Question, Product Usage Insight or  
Alert or Emerging Trends / Market Insight**.

This targeted segmentation ensures each driver is:
* 🧭 **Emotionally grounded and action-ready**
* 🔍 **Strategically routed based on intent**
* 🛠️ **Eligible for downstream orchestration (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate)**

> 🔁 **Never assign feedback type at the review level.** For multi-intent feedback, classify  
each experience driver independently.

### ✅ Key Enhancements
* ✔ **Fraud, compliance, and account access feedback is always retained — regardless of tone**  
* ✔ **Suggestions ≠ Requests** — handled with differentiated routing logic  
* ✔ **Vague, generic sentiment is excluded as noise**  
* ✔ **Mixed feedback is decomposed into distinct Experience Driver signals with separate classifications**

---

## **📍 Feedback Type Categories**

### **1️⃣ Compliment**

✅ **Definition:** Explicit **praise** for a feature, flow, or financial experience  
✔ Must highlight a **specific, meaningful strength**

✅ **Example:**

* *"The UPI AutoPay setup was incredibly smooth and fast."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This app is amazing."* (Too vague)  
* *"I love this company."* (No target entity or flow)

---

### **2️⃣ Complaint**

✅ **Definition:** Expression of **dissatisfaction, friction, or breakdown** in the customer experience  
✔ Must relate to a **fixable issue**  
✔ **Always extract** for fraud, KYC failure, refund problems, or trust violations

✅ **Examples:**

* *"My refund hasn’t come even after 10 days."*  
* *"PAN verification keeps failing even after reupload."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This app sucks."* (Too vague)  
* *"I hate this bank."* (Emotion without insight)

---

### **3️⃣ Question**

✅ **Definition:** A clear **query or request for clarification** about a process, policy, or feature  
✔ Must be a **real, answerable question** — not rhetorical or sarcastic

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Can I update my nominee online without visiting a branch?"*  
* *"Is there a daily UPI transaction limit for business accounts?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"Why is your support always useless?"* (Not a genuine question)

---

### **4️⃣ Suggestion**

✅ **Definition:** A **non-critical improvement idea** or optional enhancement  
✔ Suggests **added convenience**, not necessity

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Add biometric login for faster access."*  
* *"It would be nice to get monthly spend summaries by email."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Urgency or functionality is implied → classify as a **Request**

✅ **Handling Suggestion vs. Request**

| **User Feedback**                                                   | **Categorization** | **Reasoning**              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| *"Add dark mode to the app."*                                       | Suggestion ✅       | Cosmetic enhancement       |
| *"Allow me to download my full transaction history."*               | Suggestion ✅       | Convenience feature        |
| *"There must be an option to block the card instantly."*            | Request ✅          | Security-critical          |
| *"There should be an OTP override for low-value UPI transfers."*    | Request ✅          | Functional expectation     |

---

### **5️⃣ Request**

✅ **Definition:** A **core need** or **missing functionality** that affects the user experience  
✔ Often framed with urgency, expectation, or frustration  
✔ **Never exclude** fraud recovery, refunds, security, or KYC-related requests

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Please give an option to report fraudulent transactions from within the app."*  
* *"There should be a way to freeze UPI access temporarily."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Clearly optional feedback → classify as Suggestion

---

### **6️⃣ Product Usage Insight**

✅ **Definition:** Behavioral patterns, routines, or real-world usage notes  
✔ Helps uncover **habits or latent workflows**

✅ **Examples:**

* *"I transfer rent every month on the 1st using this app."*  
* *"I only use your app for mutual fund tracking, not for payments."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Feedback doesn’t describe real usage behavior

---

### **7️⃣ Emerging Trends / Market Insight**

✅ **Definition:** **Comparative expectations**, competitor benchmarking, or forward-looking signals  
✔ Customer is **referencing external market evolution**

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Other apps allow you to pause SIPs instantly — why don’t you?"*  
* *"Everyone now offers virtual debit cards — any plans here?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* No link to broader market comparison

---

## **📍 Key Categorization Rules**
✅ One feedback type per signal  
✅ Split compound/mixed sentiments  
✅ Always extract fraud, refund, KYC, or access-related items  
✅ Subjective or vague positivity is excluded

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Emotion**

### **📌 Purpose**
The **Emotion** dimension captures the **dominant emotional response** associated with each  
extracted **Experience Driver**. This emotion must be tied **specifically to that driver**, not to the comment overall.  
The emotional signal becomes a **core routing input** for downstream orchestration — including ERI scoring,  
stream assignment, and urgency prioritization.

✅ **Key Enhancements:**
* ✔ Emotion is extracted **per driver**, not per comment  
* ✔ One emotion per driver — the most behaviorally meaningful signal  
* ✔ All emotion labels are drawn from a **controlled taxonomy** mapped to Decipher’s **Core Emotion Framework**  
* ✔ Emotion must align with **context, feedback type, and customer tone** — no imaginative inference allowed

Use the Decipher Emotion Extraction Framework to map each emotion to one—and only one—Core Group.  
Output format includes the **core group**, the **specific emotion**, and **confidence score** if applicable.

🔎 Emotion Mapping Logic
1. **Context-First:** Read the full feedback — do not rely on emotional keywords alone

## 🎯 Extraction Guidelines

### Context-First Analysis
1. Read the full statement  
2. Identify the **specific Experience Driver** the emotion applies to  
3. Use **surrounding behavior** to infer emotional strength and valence  
4. **Avoid emotional words** without impact context (“cool,” “fine,” etc.)

---

### Precision Mapping Rules

1. **One emotion per driver**  
2. Anchor emotion to **relationship and impact**  
3. Prioritize **intensity over generic sentiment**  
4. Use **context**, not keyword counts

---

### Quality Assurance Checks

1. Does the emotion reflect the **true business risk or opportunity**?  
2. Is the **intensity justified** by the language and context?  
3. Would this emotion lead to the **correct CX action**?

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Customer Journey Classification**

### 📌 **Purpose:**
For each identified **Experience Driver**, classify the **exact moment** of the customer’s experience  
across three layers:

* **Customer Journey**,  
* **Journey Stage**, and  
* **Interaction Moment**

This ensures the **temporal and situational context** is always anchored to the **specific trigger  
that caused the experience driver to surface** — not to the general comment. This classification powers  
execution-grade orchestration by anchoring each identified experience driver to the exact when, where,  
and triggering behavior of the customer’s interaction.

✅ **Why it matters:**
* Avoids generic or averaged journey mapping across multi-driver feedback  
* Powers precision in OU formation, PDCA targeting, and root cause correlation  
* Strengthens behavioral traceability across every orchestration layer

---

Your task is to return:

1. **Customer Journey** – The broad experience phase  
   *(e.g., Onboarding & Verification, Transaction Experience, Account Maintenance)*  
2. **Journey Stage** – The specific step or interface  
   *(e.g., KYC Submission, Payment Execution, Limit Management)*  
3. **Interaction Moment** – The *exact trigger window* of emotional impact  
   *(e.g., “PAN verification failed after upload”, “Refund not initiated after failed transaction”, “UPI payment deducted twice”)*

---

### 🧠 **Classification Philosophy:**
This layer is not a category tag — it’s a behavioral timestamp that powers:  
* Diagnostic clarity (`Where & When did the emotion erupt?`)  
* Orchestration accuracy (`What exact moment must be targeted to Fix / Optimize / Amplify / Innovate?`)  
* PDCA precision (`What specific interaction triggered the customer’s feeling or suggestion?`)

🔍 Ask:
* **When** in the financial journey did this signal occur? → *Customer Journey*  
* **Where** within that phase? → *Customer Journey Stage*  
* **What micro-moment or trigger actually sparked the emotional response?** → *Interaction Moment*

> ⚠️ Classify based on when the **experience happened**, not when the feedback was shared.

---

* `Experience Driver`  
* `Entity Name`  
* `Context`  
* `Keywords`  
* `Feedback Type`  
* Any explicit or implied interfaces, events, flows, failures, or touchpoints in the verbatim

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS)**

### 📌 **Purpose**
For each extracted **Experience Driver**, determine the **single most appropriate strategic  
response pathway** — also known as the **Opportunity Maximisation Stream** (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate).  
This stream is never assigned at the comment level, but always in direct relation to the **specific issue,  
emotion, and context** tied to that experience driver.

This ensures every orchestration decision is anchored in **driver-specific behavioral logic**,  
and that system response aligns with **urgency**, **risk**, and **opportunity type**.

✅ **Why this matters:**
* Avoids overgeneralization across multi-driver feedback  
* Maintains mutual exclusivity of OMS per driver  
* Directly governs OU routing, PDCA targeting, and Silent Guidance memory triggers  
* Ensures only one OMS per Experience Driver — no overlaps or hybrids

---

This stream is not descriptive.  
It is **prescriptive** — defining the **intended business response** the system should trigger.

---

### 🧠 **OMS Selection Is Based On:**
Each signal must be evaluated using the following inputs:

* ✅ **Experience Driver** – What surface of experience is involved?  
* ✅ **Theme** – What strategic area does it fall under?  
* ✅ **Emotion** – How intense or polarized is the sentiment?  
* ✅ **Feedback Type** – Complaint, compliment, suggestion, etc.  
* ✅ **Context** – What happened? What was expected?  
* ✅ **Framing** – Does it reflect:

  * ❌ Something broken or malfunctioning?
  * ⚙️ Something usable but inefficient?
  * 🚀 Something delightful and repeatable?
  * 🧪 Something missing that the customer wants?

---

### 🛠️ **Execution Pathways Available**

Each signal must be routed into **exactly one** of the following:

| Stream       | Description                                                 |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Fix**      | Something is broken, failing, or unreliable                 |
| **Optimize** | Something works but creates confusion, delay, or friction   |
| **Amplify**  | Something works brilliantly and should be scaled            |
| **Innovate** | Something the customer wants but does not currently exist   |

⚠️ **Mutual Exclusivity Rule**: An Experience Driver can only belong to **one** stream.  
Choose based on its **core implication**, not theoretical potential.

---

### 🔒 **Why It Matters**
The OMS is the **execution-routing layer** of the Decipher system.  
It converts emotional and contextual signals into a **strategic directive** — guiding mission generation, team targeting, and learning capture.

> 🛑 No orchestration begins until a stream is assigned.

---

## 📂 The Four Streams

### 🔧 1. Fix — “Resolve what’s failing”

| Aspect              | Description                                                                               |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**       | The experience **exists**, but is **broken, failing, or blocked**                        |
| **When to Use**      | Login issues, UPI failures, refund blocks, OTP not delivered, app crashes                |
| **Signals**          | "OTP doesn’t arrive", "Refund failed", "KYC blocked after submission"                    |
| **Strict Boundary**  | ✅ Exists → ❌ Doesn’t work as expected                                                   |
| **Never Use If**     | It’s functional but clunky (→ Optimize) or nonexistent (→ Innovate)

---

### 🛠 2. Optimize — “Remove avoidable friction”

| Aspect              | Description                                                                 |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**       | The experience **works**, but creates **delay, doubt, or extra effort**     |
| **When to Use**      | Repetitive flows, confusing settings, too many steps to complete actions    |
| **Signals**          | "App makes me enter CVV every time", "Too many taps to reach statements"   |
| **Strict Boundary**  | ✅ Exists → ✅ Works → ❌ Clunky or wasteful                                 |
| **Never Use If**     | It’s broken (→ Fix) or not present (→ Innovate)

---

### 🧪 3. Innovate — “Create what’s missing”

| Aspect              | Description                                                                 |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**       | The customer requests a **feature, service, or improvement that doesn’t exist** |
| **When to Use**      | Feature requests, competitive benchmarking, emerging needs                  |
| **Signals**          | "You should allow freezing UPI access", "Why not offer virtual cards?"      |
| **Strict Boundary**  | ❌ Not currently offered — **net-new build required**                        |
| **Never Use If**     | It’s refinement of something that exists (→ Optimize) or broken (→ Fix)

✅ **Only Innovate** supports **Sub-Tier Innovation Scoring**:

| Score | Tier Label              | Description                                                                 |
|-------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Hygiene Need            | Fulfills a basic digital expectation (e.g. card controls, mini-statements) |
| 2     | Convenience Enhancer    | Reduces steps or effort (e.g. biometric login, favorites shortcut)         |
| 3     | Experience Upgrade      | Improves UX delight (e.g. spend charts, gamified saving goals)             |
| 4     | New Capability          | Adds powerful utility (e.g. goal-based investing, instant EMI conversion)  |
| 5     | Transformational Shift  | Redefines customer interaction (e.g. AI co-pilot, proactive fraud sweeps)  |

---

### 📣 4. Amplify — “Scale what earns love”

| Aspect              | Description                                                                   |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**       | The customer highlights something working **exceptionally well**              |
| **When to Use**      | Support praise, real-time alerts, smooth onboarding, best-in-class UX         |
| **Signals**          | "Fraud alert saved me", "Login is fast and reliable", "KYC was super easy"    |
| **Strict Boundary**  | ✅ Exists → ✅ Works exceptionally well                                        |
| **Never Use If**     | Any signal of improvement, failure, or feature request is present

---

## 🔍 Diagnostic Flowchart

```text
Does it exist?
├── No → INNOVATE
│
├── Yes → Is it broken?
│     ├── Yes → FIX
│     └── No → Is it inefficient or confusing?
│             ├── Yes → OPTIMIZE
│             └── No → Is it delightful?
│                     ├── Yes → AMPLIFY
│                     └── No → Discard or mark unclear
````

## 🧠 Execution Rules
* Assign **exactly one** stream per Experience Driver
* Never blur the lines between Fix and Optimize
* Use **Sub-Tier Scoring only for Innovate**
* Stream assignment must be **explainable, auditable, and based on structured fields**

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Customer Effort Score (CES)**

### 📌 **Purpose**
The **Customer Effort Score (CES)** measures how easy or difficult it was for the customer to
engage with a specific **Experience Driver**. Rather than scoring the full comment, CES is assigned
**per experience driver**, capturing the **local friction or fluency** experienced in relation to that
dimension only.

It uses a **1–7 scale**, with 1 being **effortless** and 7 indicating **extreme friction or complexity**.
The score is computed from both:

- The **Emotion** dimension (perceived emotional effort)
- The **Opportunity Maximisation Stream** (functional or strategic classification)

✅ **Why this matters:**
* Disentangles multiple friction zones in multi-driver feedback
* Supports urgency triage, especially in borderline ERI × RF edge cases
* Feeds into the behavioral impact summary within each Orchestration Unit

---

### ✅ CES Enables:

- **Effort-Based Prioritization**: Identify and resolve high-friction issues.
- **Strategic Experience Mapping**: Align effort with journey stages and KPIs.
- **Friction Reduction Programs**: Optimize for seamless customer journeys.

---

## **CES Scoring Scale & Interpretation**

| **CES Score** | **Effort Level** | **User Perception** | **Business Implication** |
|---------------|------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| **1**         | Very Low Effort  | Effortless, intuitive, delightful | Strength to **amplify** as a CX differentiator |
| **2–3**       | Low Effort       | Minimal friction, smooth process  | Positive CX, **low-priority optimization** |
| **4**         | Moderate Effort  | Some friction, but task completed | Opportunity to **innovate** experience |
| **5–6**       | High Effort      | Delays, retries, confusion         | **Fix required** to prevent churn or frustration |
| **7**         | Very High Effort | Severe breakdown, task failure     | **Critical fix** required, high risk of churn |

---

## **CES Decision Framework**

---

### **1️⃣ Assign Initial CES Based on Emotion**

| **Emotion Category** | **Default Effort Perception** | **CES Range** |
|----------------------|-------------------------------|---------------|
| **Adoration**        | Very Low Effort               | 1             |
| **Appreciation**     | Low Effort                    | 2–3           |
| **Ambivalence**      | Moderate Effort               | 4             |
| **Agitation**        | High Effort                   | 5–6           |
| **Anger**            | Very High Effort              | 7             |

> 🚨 **Constraint**: Emotion alone gives a **starting range** — actual CES is finalized after considering the **Actionable Category**.

---

### **2️⃣ Adjust CES Based on Actionable Category**

| **Actionable Category** | CES Adjustment | Allowed CES Range |
|-------------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Amplify**             | Lock to Low     | 1–3               |
| **Optimize**            | Lock to Low-Moderate | 3–4         |
| **Innovate**            | Lock to Moderate | 4                 |
| **Fix**                 | Lock to High     | 5–7               |

🚨 **Hard Rules:**
- **Amplify** → CES must be **1, 2, or 3**
- **Optimize** → CES must be **3 or 4**
- **Innovate** → CES must be **4**
- **Fix** → CES must be **5, 6, or 7**

---

### **3️⃣ Refine CES Based on Feedback Context**

Use the following rules to assign a final value **within the locked range**:

#### 🚀 **Assign a Higher CES (Within Allowed Range) If:**
- The user experienced **prolonged effort, retries, or confusion**
- There is **emotional distress or delay anxiety**
- The issue **blocked a critical journey stage** (e.g., checkout, login, refund)

#### 📉 **Assign a Lower CES (Within Allowed Range) If:**
- The task was **eventually completed** despite friction
- There was a **workaround or partial resolution**
- The complaint was **mild or not disruptive**

---

### **4️⃣ CES Escalation Rules (Override Mechanism)**

- ✅ **CES must be `"7"`** if the task **could not be completed** and **no workaround was available**
- ✅ **CES must be `"6"`** if task **was completed** but with **major frustration or failure risk**
- ✅ **CES must be `"5"`** if the process was **inefficient but manageable**

---

### **5️⃣ CES Output Format**

✅ Always return CES as a **quoted number**:

"Customer Effort Score": "6"

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Orchestration Unit (OU) Construction** (FinTech Edition)

### 🎯 **Purpose**
An **Orchestration Unit (OU)** in FinTech is a precision-guided micro-mission built from structured feedback, 
focused on a distinct **Experience Driver** tied to:

> **Emotion × Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream**

It blends regulatory sensitivity, customer trust, financial complexity, and emotional signal routing into 
one execution-grade payload. Each OU includes:

* Feedback Type
* Opportunity Stream (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate)
* Interaction Moment
* **Matters** (behavioral statement)

---

## 🔍 **Matters – Behavioral Trigger Extraction (God-Tier Edition)**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
**Matters** distills the *behaviorally critical moment* in the customer’s experience — the precise interaction, breakdown, or unmet need that emotionally and functionally *mattered*.

This is not a summary. It is not a diagnosis.

It is the **customer’s lived moment of friction or desire**, captured with execution-grade precision.

In FinTech, this includes:
* **Moments of confusion** over limits, fees, terms
* **Failures of trust** in transaction or identity systems
* **Invisible effort** hidden in security loops or manual escalations
* **Missed personalization** tied to usage behavior or financial profile
* **Delays or breakdowns** in money movement, assurance, or status clarity

---

### 🧠 **Construction Protocol**
Matters must be **behaviorally precise**, **emotionally traceable**, and **execution-ready**. Each statement should reflect a **trigger** that would justify either fixing, optimizing, innovating, or amplifying an experience.

---

### 🧬 **Extraction Hierarchy – Strict Priority**

Pull from these sources *in order*, stopping at the highest fidelity input that clearly expresses what happened:
1. **Raw Customer Voice** – exact phrasing from the verbatim
2. **Context** – narrative explanation of the moment
3. **Keywords** – friction indicators or emotional anchors
4. **Entity Name** – only if embedded with meaning (e.g., “App Freeze”)
5. **Journey Stage** – to frame when/where the issue emerged
6. **Feedback Type** – for tone pattern, *not content*

---

### ✅ **Rules of Output**
* Always use **behavioral phrasing** (e.g., "manual dispute process delays resolution", "invisible effort in ID re-verification").
* Capture **what the user went through**, not what the business did wrong.
* Do **not** paraphrase the whole experience.
* One sharp, stand-alone sentence — maximum **2 clauses** if absolutely needed.

---

### ✨ **Examples (Derived from Your Latest God-Level Feedback)**

* **Bad**: "The customer was frustrated by app reliability."
  ✅ **Better**: "App freezes during fund transfer break transaction flow and create fear of failed payments."

* **Bad**: "They didn’t know they could upgrade their card."
  ✅ **Better**: "Lack of proactive card upgrade prompts keeps users locked in suboptimal usage tiers."

* **Bad**: "Customer felt supported."
  ✅ **Better**: "Rep-led support follow-ups show users they are remembered, not just resolved."

---

### 🧠 **FinTech-Aligned MATTERS Templates by Feedback Type**

| **Feedback Type**                    | **Pattern Template**                                                               | **Example**                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Complaint**                        | `[financial failure or error] that [caused loss, delay, or distrust]`              | `payment freeze that blocked access to emergency funds`                     |
| **Compliment**                       | `[feature, staff, or experience] that [reinforced security or trust]`              | `instant fraud alerts that built confidence in account protection`          |
| **Request**                          | `[missing feature or control] that [limits financial freedom or confidence]`       | `lack of limit-setting on cards that reduces spending control`              |
| **Suggestion**                       | `[improvement idea] that [could enhance financial clarity or ease]`                | `adding transaction categories that would simplify budgeting`               |
| **Question**                         | `[unclear policy or decision] that [triggered confusion or doubt]`                 | `ambiguous credit score impact of loan inquiry that raised concern`         |
| **Product Usage Insight**            | `[routine financial behavior] that [signals trust or preference]`                  | `daily use of app to check balances shows high reliance on mobile banking`  |
| **Emerging Trends / Market Insight** | `[external fintech shift or norm] that [creates expectation or pressure to adapt]` | `rival banks offering instant pay refunds that raise expectations of speed` |

## 🕓 **Interaction Moment – Temporal Precision (FinTech Edition)**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
Pinpoint the exact **event or action** that triggered the feedback — must be system-anchored and emotionally resonant.

Typical **FinTech Interaction Moments**:

* while authenticating identity
* during bill payment confirmation
* after receiving fraud alert
* while reviewing account statement
* during KYC/AML verification
* while checking transaction limits
* when trying to freeze a card
* while scheduling an auto-payment

✅ Must be:
*Time-specific* → *Execution-locatable* → *Emotionally traceable*

## 💬 **Context – Customer’s Financial Experience**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
**Context** captures the **precise financial action or system state** experienced by the user. It’s never 
abstract or vague.

🧠 **Section 1 Fusion Logic (FinTech)**:
Blend **Interaction Moment** with **Context** using tight causal phrasing.

✅ FinTech Examples:
* The customer submitted a complaint that when attempting to verify their identity, the system failed to recognize valid documents.
* The user made a suggestion that during bill payment setup, the app did not allow variable amounts per cycle.

❌ Avoid:

* “...during login they had issue...” → Incomplete cause chain
* “...user saw error message and...” → Ambiguous flow

## ✅ **Matters Quality Criteria (FinTech Edition)**

| ✔ Attribute         | ✅ What It Means                                                             |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Behavioral**      | Describes a direct customer action or attempt (e.g., payment, freeze, view) |
| **Specific**        | Anchored to 1–2 steps, not generalized “app issues”                         |
| **Customer-Led**    | Reflects frustration, delight, confusion **in financial terms**             |
| **Execution-Ready** | Tech, Ops, Compliance, or Risk teams can act on it directly                 |

| `login problem`               | Ambiguous – not actionable      | `OTP expired too quickly during device login process`        |

## 🔐 **SEMANTIC VALIDATION PROTOCOL – FinTech Variant**
To ensure **only strategic, action-worthy OUs** enter execution and memory, each must pass:

---

### ✅ **1. Singular & Distinct**
> One Experience Driver × One Opportunity Stream × One Feedback Type

e.g.,
**“ATM Access → Fix → Complaint”** with behaviorally distinct Matters

---

### ✅ **2. Behaviorally Anchored**
Is the MATTERS statement **observable**, not inferred?

Bad:
> "poor customer experience during account setup"

Good:
> "address mismatch error during video KYC that blocked onboarding"

---

### ✅ **3. Execution-Ready**
Can this be sent to Product, Risk, or Tech **without needing interpretation**?

Bad:
> "some glitch in system" → no action possible

Good:
> "auto-debit triggered on paused subscription"

---

### ✅ **4. Feedback-Aligned**
Does the **Feedback Type** match the **moment + context**?

e.g., Don’t tag it a “suggestion” if the user is venting frustration — it’s likely a “complaint”

---

### ✅ **5. Semantically Complete**
Both sections of the OU must work as one unit:

```
SECTION 1: [Feedback Type] → [Interaction Moment] → [Context]
SECTION 2: [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] → [Matters]
```

Example:

```
The customer filed a complaint that when attempting to update their mobile number, the system required a branch visit without explanation.

We need to Fix → Account Access Controls in response to a Complaint reflecting Agitation about identity updates requiring physical branch visits without in-app alternatives. This matters because it breaks digital expectations and wastes customer time.
```

## 🧩 **ORCHESTRATION UNIT FORMAT**

* **OU Name**: `[Entity Name] – [Feedback Type] – [Opportunity Stream]`
* **Semantic Action Statement**:

  ```
  SECTION 1: THE CUSTOMER REALITY
 The customer [submitted/made/gave] a [Feedback Type] that during [Interaction Moment], [Context]

  SECTION 2: THE STRATEGIC RESPONSE
  We need to [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] to address [MATTERS]
  ```

* **Opportunity Stream**: `Fix | Optimize | Innovate | Amplify`
* **Experience Driver**: `Category → Subcategory`
* **Feedback Type**: `Complaint | Compliment | Request | Suggestion | Question | Product Usage Insight | Emerging Trend`
* **Matters**: `Direct behavioral trigger described in plain, precise language`
* **Interaction Moment**: `Time/trigger of the experience`
* **Context**: `What actually happened — pulled directly from customer reality`
* **Stream Justification**: `Why this stream is correct based on feedback type, behavior, and urgency`
* **Matters Extraction**: `Source(s) used → e.g., Voice, Context, Keywords`
* **Behavioral Impact**: `What this OU aims to shift — trust, ease, friction, adoption, retention, etc.`

## 🧠 **ADVANCED SEMANTIC PATTERNS**

### 🧷 **Multi-Entity Input**
Always build OUs around the **Experience Driver explicitly tied to the customer’s feedback intent**.

> *If the review mentions both checkout and delivery, but the **request** is anchored on delivery —
> then the correct OU is: `Delivery Experience → Timing`, not `Checkout Flow → Summary`.*

---

### 🌊 **Causal Accuracy**
Anchor the OU to the **source behavior** (what actually happened), not the surface outcome (what resulted).

| ❌ Incorrect Focus                               | ✅ Correct Focus                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `checkout failures that cause cart abandonment` | `unclear payment validation that disrupts confidence during transaction flow` |
| `late delivery that caused meal issues`         | `failure to honor delivery time slots that affected planned meal preparation` |

---

### 🚀 **Innovation & Amplify Stream Rules**
These streams require **future-oriented, behaviorally-grounded phrasing** — they are **generative**, not reactive.

* **Section 1 (Customer Reality)**
  > *“The customer provided a Suggestion that during [Interaction Moment] they wanted...”*

* **Section 2 (Strategic Response)**
  > *“We need to Amplify → [Experience Driver] to address [behavioral value] that enhances [engagement/outcome metric].”*

✅ Avoid vague expressions like: “make it better”, “more features”, “general improvement”.

## 🧬 **NON-NEGOTIABLE SEMANTIC LAWS**

## 🧩 **Orchestration Unit – SEMANTIC COMPONENTS**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
An **Orchestration Unit (OU)** is a **micro-mission** constructed from a unique semantic signature:

> **Experience Driver × Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream**

This signature is translated into a **two-part instruction payload** — designed for:

* 📤 **Execution Routing**
* 🧠 **Institutional Archiving (IOA)**
* 🔁 **Strategic Recall & Nudging (SG)**

---

## 🔐 **Canonical Two-Part Format**

```
SECTION 1: THE CUSTOMER REALITY
The customer submitted a [Feedback Type] during [Interaction Moment], [Context]

SECTION 2: THE STRATEGIC RESPONSE
We need to [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] to address [MATTERS]
```

This structure ensures:
* 🎯 Behavioral traceability
* ⏱ Temporal precision
* 🚀 Execution-grade clarity

---

## ⚙️ **Section 1 – Feedback Intent × Trigger Moment × Observed Context**

This section answers:
* **Why** did the customer speak?
* **When** did it happen?
* **What** exactly occurred?

| Component              | Description                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Feedback Type**      | One of the 7 canonical forms: Complaint, Compliment, Request, Suggestion, etc. |
| **Interaction Moment** | The exact point in the experience when the feedback was triggered              |
| **Context**            | The direct, observable event that the customer encountered — no inference      |

✅ Keep phrasing **literal**, **precise**, and **anchored to the customer’s situation**.

---

## 🎯 **Section 2 – Action Stream × Experience Driver × Behavioral Justification**
This section defines the **strategic response** the organization should take — grounded directly in what the 
customer expressed.

---

### 🔁 **[Opportunity Stream] – Directive Purpose**

Select **one** stream. It defines the **strategic intention** of the OU:

| Stream       | Purpose                                                     |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fix**      | Resolve broken, failing, or misleading experiences          |
| **Optimize** | Improve inefficient or effort-heavy workflows               |
| **Innovate** | Build new features or flows based on emerging needs         |
| **Amplify**  | Reinforce praise, delight, or emotionally rich interactions |

🔹 Use this **exact phrasing** in Section 2:

> `We need to [Stream] → [Experience Driver] to address...`

---

### 📍 **[Experience Driver] – Tactical Execution Anchor**

Use the precise extracted form in the format:

> `"Category → Subcategory"`
✅ No rewrites.
✅ No compression.
✅ No reinterpretation.

| ✅ Correct                               | ❌ Incorrect         |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `"Delivery Experience → Timing"`        | `"Late deliveries"` |
| `"Digital Checkout → Promo Redemption"` | `"Promo issues"`    |

## 🔍 **Matters – Behavioral Justification Protocol**

### 🎯 **Purpose**
Matters is the **behavioral anchor** of the Orchestration Unit — the sharp, execution-ready articulation of what tangibly happened to the customer. It captures the **trigger moment**, not a summary or diagnosis.

This is the bridge between emotion and action. It is what makes the feedback *orchestratable*.

---

### 🧠 **Core Principles**

A valid Matters must be:

✅ **Behavior-Specific** – rooted in a clear user action or expectation
✅ **Directly Observable** – surfaced from verbatim, context, or keywords
✅ **Free of Abstraction** – no generic UX jargon or sentiment fluff
✅ **Execution-Ready** – usable by a product or ops team *without interpretation*

---

### 📏 **Matters Construction Hierarchy**

Extract in strict priority from:
1. **Raw Customer Voice**
2. **Context Field**
3. **Keywords List**
4. **Entity Name** (only if behavioral)
5. **Journey Stage** (to clarify when, not what)
6. **Feedback Type** (for tone scaffolding only)

---

### 🧬 **Behavioral Construction Rules**
* Format: **One sharp, specific sentence** (ideally 8–16 words)
* Tone: **Behavioral**, not emotional or strategic
* Frame: What the user **did, experienced, or expected**
* No: Vague phrases like “UX problem,” “confusion,” “inefficiency,” or “unpleasant experience”

---
