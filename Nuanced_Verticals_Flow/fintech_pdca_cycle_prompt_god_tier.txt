plan:
  problem_statement: >
    The customer expressed anger after receiving duplicate debit notifications for a single credit card payment. 
    Although one of the charges was later reversed, the experience caused immediate panic and fear of account 
    compromise. The orchestration stream is Fix — intended to eliminate volatile transaction errors that destabilize 
    user trust. This occurred during Payment Processing in the Transaction Experience journey. The issue was routed 
    here because even momentary transaction anomalies shatter the fragile trust users place in digital financial systems. 
    If left unaddressed, it may trigger defensive behaviors like freezing cards or switching platforms, and erode 
    long-term digital banking confidence.

  five_whys:
    - Why did the customer panic? → They received two debit alerts for the same credit card payment
    - Why did they receive duplicate alerts? → The transaction system issued redundant confirmation messages
    - Why was the system allowed to do that? → There is no anomaly detection layer to flag or suppress duplicates
    - Why hasn’t anomaly detection been implemented? → Historical monitoring focused on transaction success rates, not customer perception patterns
    - Why was customer perception deprioritized? → Monitoring KPIs were designed around backend reconciliation accuracy, not frontend experience fidelity

  cross_domain_validation:
    - domain: Technology and Innovation
      insight: Real-time anomaly suppression is absent from the event notification system
    - domain: Customer Service and Support
      insight: Users are forced to verify issues manually, increasing support load and emotional volatility
    - domain: Compliance and Regulatory Exposure
      insight: Repeated debit alerts without confirmed failure may be interpreted as unauditable or misleading billing behavior

  systemic_implications:
    - Transaction monitoring frameworks are backend-biased and may overlook emotionally critical UX-layer signals
    - Alerting logic is tightly coupled with system state, not customer experience, increasing perception-risk divergence

  diagnostic_epiphany: >
    The customer’s panic wasn’t about being charged — it was about not knowing whether they were. 
    The emotional breach occurred not in the backend transaction flow, but in the frontend trust layer.

    thought to monitor the notification experience. This isn’t just a bug in alerts — it’s a failure to 
    protect the emotional contract that underpins every digital transaction. In financial UX, doubt is as

## ✅ Check Phase - Prioritization Matrix
You are now in the Check Phase of the PDCA cycle.

Your task is to evaluate and prioritize the initiatives generated in the Do Phase using the standardized 
scoring framework below.

Do not reinterpret the problem, root cause, or stream.  
This is a mechanical triage process to determine which initiatives are most actionable, impactful, and 
strategically fit for execution.

Score each initiative independently. Use the defined rules to assign values across Impact, Feasibility, 
Alignment, and Risk.

---

### 🎯 Scoring Framework

For each proposed initiative, score based on four key criteria: Impact, Feasibility, Alignment, and Risk.
The higher the score, the higher the priority. Each initiative should be scored on a 1-5 scale for each criterion.

📘 Continue below for the full scoring framework:

### 🧠 Incorporating Innovation Potential

The `innovation_score` (from the Do Phase) reflects how forward-thinking or transformative the 
initiative is — but it **does not change the Total Score directly**.

Instead:
- Use it to validate high Impact scores in Innovate or Optimise streams.
- If Innovation Score is high (4–5) but Feasibility is low, **still consider it strategically 
valuable** (e.g., pilot instead of deprioritizing).
- Fix initiatives can have innovation too — e.g., “AI-based root cause monitor” instead of 
“update SOP.”

### 🧮 Total Score Formula:
```plaintext
Total Score = (Impact + Feasibility + Alignment) – Risk

| Total Score | Priority Level                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **10–15**   | High Priority – Must action           |
| **6–9**     | Medium Priority – Worth doing / pilot |
| **0–5**     | Low Priority – Deprioritize for now   |

---

📛 Mandatory Rules:
- Do not overinflate scores. Apply each dimension independently.
- Use emotion and root cause context to justify Impact and Risk.
- Business Division must influence Feasibility and Alignment scores.
- Total scores must never exceed 15.
- Do not use innovation_score as a substitute for impact or feasibility.

Each score should help the business triage what to act on, what to monitor, and what to park.

## ✅ Act Phase – Business Outcomes & Commitment

Following the identification and prioritization of initiatives of the DO PHASE AND CHECK PHASE, the final step 
is to clearly define the strategic business outcomes that each initiative is expected to achieve. These 
outcomes must reflect the opportunity maximisation streams (Fix, Optimise, Innovate, Amplify) and address 
the root cause analysis findings of the PLAN PHASE.

Each outcome must clearly articulate the intended business benefit of the initiative and be grounded in the 
original root cause diagnosis from the Plan Phase. Outcomes must not only reflect the opportunity stream 
intent (Fix, Optimize, Innovate, Amplify) but also provide a traceable link to emotional telemetry, 
customer journey stage, and operational readiness. Every KPI must be SMART, owner-trackable, and directly 
connected to measurable change.

Outcomes should be organized under relevant strategic pillars, ensuring that they reflect both short-term 
operational needs and long-term strategic goals. Key areas of impact include customer experience, 
operational efficiency, financial performance, and innovation.

*Select 1–3 pillars that best align with your initiative's intent, emotional signals, journey impact, and 
business objectives*

**Focus:** Improving how customers feel, act, or perceive value

### Key Outcomes:
- Reduce customer confusion or frustration
- Improve usability, accessibility, or experience flow
- Enhance emotional connection or brand trust
- Raise NPS, CSAT, CRR, or CES scores

(not backend processes)

### Success Metrics:
- Customer satisfaction scores
- Task completion rates
- Time-to-value metrics
- Support ticket reduction

## 📏 KPI Requirements (Non-Negotiable – No Hallucinations Allowed)
**Your task is to define success in hard numbers.**

Every initiative must have at least one SMART KPI — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound — that:

🎯 **Directly reflects what will improve if the initiative succeeds**  
🏢 **Is actionable and trackable by the assigned likely_owner**  
🔍 **Can be monitored quantitatively by a real-world analytics or ops team**  
🚫 **Must NOT be vague, fabricated, abstract, or hypothetical**

### 🚨 Mandatory Rules:
❌ **Do NOT invent fictional metrics** like "Customer Joy Quotient"  
❌ **Do NOT use qualitative phrases** like "Better Experience" as KPIs  
✅ **Always pick from real-world, business-used metrics**  
✅ **Match KPI to strategic pillar and execution owner**

### 📊 Pillar-to-KPI Alignment

| **Strategic Pillar** | **Valid Example KPIs** |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| **Customer-Centricity** | CSAT, NPS, CRR (Customer Retention Rate), CES (Effort Score), FCR (First Contact Resolution), Time to Resolution |
| **Operational Excellence** | SLA Compliance, AHT (Average Handle Time), Order Accuracy, Error Rate, Downtime |
| **Financial Performance** | Revenue Growth, Gross Margin, CLV (Customer Lifetime Value), ARPU, CAC |
| **Innovation & Transformation** | Tech Adoption Rate, New Feature Usage, Time to Market, % Self-Service Conversion |
| **Brand Differentiation** | Brand Sentiment (via social/NPS), Social Engagement Rate, Share of Voice |
| **Sustainable Growth** | Repeat Purchase Rate, Net Revenue Retention (NRR), Upsell Rate |
| **Risk Management & Compliance** | Audit Success Rate, SLA Breach Frequency, Compliance Score, Reported Risk Incidents |

### 📘 How to Choose the Right KPI
**Use the intersection of these 3 criteria:**

#### 1. What the Initiative Improves
Does it remove pain, increase delight, reduce cost, increase adoption, reduce churn, or reinforce the brand?

#### 2. What the likely_owner Can Deliver On
E.g., an Ops Manager can reduce downtime — but shouldn't be assigned Brand NPS.

#### 3. Where in the Journey the Impact is Felt
Match the KPI's scope to the journey stage of the OU (e.g., checkout, onboarding, post-purchase, etc.)

    The customer expressed anger after receiving duplicate debit notifications for a single credit card payment. 
    Although one of the charges was later reversed, the experience caused immediate panic and fear of account 
    compromise. The orchestration stream is Fix — intended to eliminate volatile transaction errors that destabilize 
    user trust. This occurred during Payment Processing in the Transaction Experience journey. The issue was routed 
    here because even momentary transaction anomalies shatter the fragile trust users place in digital financial systems. 
    If left unaddressed, it may trigger defensive behaviors like freezing cards or switching platforms, and erode 
    long-term digital banking confidence.

  five_whys:
    - Why did the customer panic? → They received two debit alerts for the same credit card payment
    - Why did they receive duplicate alerts? → The transaction system issued redundant confirmation messages
    - Why was the system allowed to do that? → There is no anomaly detection layer to flag or suppress duplicates
    - Why hasn’t anomaly detection been implemented? → Historical monitoring focused on transaction success rates, not customer perception patterns
    - Why was customer perception deprioritized? → Monitoring KPIs were designed around backend reconciliation accuracy, not frontend experience fidelity

  cross_domain_validation:
    - domain: Technology and Innovation
      insight: Real-time anomaly suppression is absent from the event notification system
    - domain: Customer Service and Support
      insight: Users are forced to verify issues manually, increasing support load and emotional volatility
    - domain: Compliance and Regulatory Exposure
      insight: Repeated debit alerts without confirmed failure may be interpreted as unauditable or misleading billing behavior

  systemic_implications:
    - Transaction monitoring frameworks are backend-biased and may overlook emotionally critical UX-layer signals
    - Alerting logic is tightly coupled with system state, not customer experience, increasing perception-risk divergence

  diagnostic_epiphany: >
    The customer’s panic wasn’t about being charged — it was about not knowing whether they were. 
    The emotional breach occurred not in the backend transaction flow, but in the frontend trust layer. 
    What should’ve been a seamless, silent moment of success turned into perceived fraud because no one 
    thought to monitor the notification experience. This isn’t just a bug in alerts — it’s a failure to 
    protect the emotional contract that underpins every digital transaction. In financial UX, doubt is as 
    dangerous as error.
