Each extraction must also include:

* A corresponding **Theme** (strategic or operational healthcare context)
* A specific **Entity Name** (verbatim or interpreted reference within the driver — e.g., “CityCare Telehealth Portal”)

* ❌ Freestyle category creation
* ❌ Abstract or overly broad labels
* ❌ Tagging Theme or Entity Name as the Experience Driver

# **Experience Driver – Specific Context (Healthcare Edition)**

### 📌 **Purpose**

The **Experience Driver Context** provides a **precise and actionable explanation** for **why a specific Experience Driver is being mentioned** in the patient, caregiver, or healthcare consumer feedback. It isolates the **core trigger, patient challenge, or improvement opportunity** linked to that driver — ensuring that each Experience Driver is interpreted **independently**, even if multiple are present in a single review or case note.

This targeted context becomes the foundational layer for:

* 🎯 **Opportunity Stream Routing**
* 📉 **Impact and Risk Assessment**
* 🧠 **Downstream orchestration logic (e.g., OU generation, PDCA planning)**

> 🔁 **Do not generalize across the entire feedback record.** The context must be anchored exclusively to the **specific Experience Driver** being evaluated.

✅ **Key Enhancements**
✔ Context must reflect a **specific, measurable impact** on the patient or care process
✔ **Actionability is non-negotiable** — vague or neutral statements are excluded
✔ Special priority handling for **clinical safety, compliance, and access-to-care risks**
✔ Distinct feedback threads must produce **distinct Experience Drivers** if outcomes differ

---

## **📍 Context Extraction Guidelines**

### **1️⃣ Relevance and Specificity**

✅ **Rule:** Extract only if the feedback **explicitly links the Experience Driver to a patient-impacting moment**
✔ Context must clarify how the experience affected the patient’s care, access, or wellbeing (positively or negatively)
✔ Exclude generic, passive, or unimpactful remarks

✅ **Include:**

* "Appointment was cancelled on the same day without notifying me."
* "Lab results were uploaded two days late, delaying my treatment."
* "Telehealth session dropped twice during my consultation."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The hospital is fine." (No actionable value)
* "I like the clinic building." (Too vague)
* "Healthcare is slow." (Subjective, lacks specificity)

---

### **2️⃣ Keywords Extraction**

✅ **Rule:** Extract **specific operational, clinical, or emotional terms** that connect the context to a clear problem or praise point
✔ Keywords must anchor the Experience Driver to real-world care friction or delight

✅ **Example:**

| **Context**                                                   | **Extracted Keywords**                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| "My MRI results were misplaced and I had to retake the scan." | "Lost records", "MRI duplication", "Admin error" |
| "The specialist spent extra time explaining my diagnosis."    | "Patient education", "Extended consultation"     |
| "The appointment booking system crashed during scheduling."   | "Booking failure", "System outage"               |

---

### **3️⃣ Actionable Depth**

✅ **Rule:** Context must contain **enough clarity** to enable an action or fix
✔ Avoid retaining emotional complaints without a clear anchor
✔ Keep anything that highlights a correctable or improvable moment

✅ **Include:**

* "Prescribed medication was out of stock and no alternative was suggested."
* "Insurance claim was denied without clear explanation."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "Hospitals are frustrating." (No causality)
* "This clinic is not for me." (No specificity)

---

### **4️⃣ Handling Multi-Dimensional Drivers**

✅ **Rule:** If the same Experience Driver has **multiple actionable outcomes**, extract each one separately
✔ Never collapse separate sentiments under one context

✅ **Example:**
**Feedback:** “The doctor was very attentive, but the billing took over an hour.”

✔ **Extracted Contexts:**

* Context 1: "Doctor was very attentive."
* Context 2: "Billing took over an hour."

---

### **5️⃣ Business-Critical Priority Override** *(Clinical Safety, Regulatory Compliance, Access Barriers)*

✅ **Rule:** Always extract feedback that surfaces **safety risks, compliance breaches, or care access limitations**, even if the language is imprecise
✔ These categories override strict context thresholds

✅ **Examples:**

* "The wrong patient file was shown during my consultation." → ✅ Extract (Safety Risk)
* "The clinic is not wheelchair accessible." → ✅ Extract (Accessibility)
* "Lab shared my medical records with another patient." → ✅ Extract (Data Privacy Violation)

---

### **6️⃣ Strict Non-Actionable Context Exclusion**

✅ **Rule:** If the Experience Driver is valid but the context offers **no insight**, discard the instance
✔ Only extract if there’s **clear cause or opportunity**

✅ **Include:**

* "Surgery date was moved without consulting me."
* "Discharge summary contained errors in medication dosage."

❌ **Exclude:**

* "The hospital is decent."
* "No issues so far."

---

## **📍 Enhanced Documentation Format**

✅ Each extracted Experience Driver must include the following structured fields:

✔ **Experience Driver**: [Identified Named Experience Driver]
✔ **Context**: [Specific and actionable text tied to the Experience Driver]
✔ **Keywords**: [Anchoring terms that expose the root of the issue or praise]

## **7️⃣ Stricter Handling of Vague Contexts**

### **1️⃣ Insufficient Context**

❌ **Exclude:**

* "Visiting the clinic is frustrating." → No actionable reason provided

✅ **Include:**

✅ **Rule:** Merge duplicates only if insights are identical

* "Nurse was caring during surgery prep but unavailable during post-op recovery." → ✅ Extract both separately

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Feedback Type**

### 📌 **Purpose**

The **Feedback Type** dimension is applied to each extracted Experience Driver.
It classifies the **intent, tone, and patient expectation** linked to that specific driver — whether it reflects a
**Complaint, Suggestion, Compliment, Request, Question, Usage Insight, or Emerging Trends / Market Insight**.

This targeted segmentation ensures each driver is:

* 🧭 **Emotionally grounded and action-ready**
* 🔍 **Strategically routed based on intent**
* 🛠️ **Eligible for downstream orchestration (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate)**

> 🔁 **Never assign feedback type at the review level.** For multi-intent feedback, classify each experience driver independently.

### ✅ Key Enhancements

* ✔ **Critical care, medication safety, and appointment access feedback is always retained — regardless of tone**
* ✔ **Suggestions ≠ Requests** — handled with differentiated routing logic
* ✔ **Vague, generic sentiment is excluded as noise**
* ✔ **Mixed feedback is decomposed into distinct Experience Driver signals with separate classifications**

---

## **📍 Feedback Type Categories**

### **1️⃣ Compliment**

✅ **Definition:** Explicit **praise** for a clinical service, care process, staff behavior, or healthcare experience
✔ Must highlight a **specific, meaningful strength**

✅ **Example:**

* *"The nurse explained my post-surgery care instructions clearly and patiently."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This hospital is great."* (Too vague)
* *"I love this place."* (No target service or process)

---

### **2️⃣ Complaint**

✅ **Definition:** Expression of **dissatisfaction, friction, or breakdown** in the patient journey
✔ Must relate to a **fixable issue**
✔ **Always extract** for medication errors, delayed care, access barriers, billing disputes, or patient safety concerns

✅ **Examples:**

* *"My test results took over a week when I was told two days."*
* *"The online appointment system kept crashing and I missed my slot."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"This clinic is useless."* (Too vague)
* *"I hate this hospital."* (Emotion without insight)

---

### **3️⃣ Question**

✅ **Definition:** A clear **query or request for clarification** about treatment, policy, process, or coverage
✔ Must be a **real, answerable question** — not rhetorical or sarcastic

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Can I get my lab reports emailed directly to me?"*
* *"Is the flu vaccine available for children under 5 here?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* *"Why do you never answer calls?"* (Not a genuine question)

---

### **4️⃣ Suggestion**

✅ **Definition:** A **non-critical improvement idea** or optional enhancement
✔ Suggests **added convenience**, not a core safety or access requirement

✅ **Examples:**

* *"It would be nice to have a quiet waiting room for post-op patients."*
* *"Add digital check-in kiosks to speed up registration."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Urgency or essential care is implied → classify as a **Request**

✅ **Handling Suggestion vs. Request**

| **Patient Feedback**                                                    | **Categorization** | **Reasoning**             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| *"Add herbal tea options in recovery ward."*                            | Suggestion ✅       | Comfort enhancement       |
| *"Provide a wellness newsletter with preventive tips."*                 | Suggestion ✅       | Informational convenience |
| *"There must be an emergency hotline for after-hours pediatric cases."* | Request ✅          | Safety-critical           |
| *"There should be an online prescription renewal option."*              | Request ✅          | Functional expectation    |

---

### **5️⃣ Request**

✅ **Definition:** A **core need** or **missing functionality** that affects care quality, safety, or patient access
✔ Often framed with urgency, expectation, or frustration
✔ **Never exclude** medication safety, urgent care access, test result delivery, or insurance approval requests

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Please allow appointment booking for multiple specialists in one visit."*
* *"There should be a 24/7 pharmacy on-site for discharged patients."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Clearly optional → classify as Suggestion

---

### **6️⃣ Usage Insight**

✅ **Definition:** Behavioral patterns, care routines, or real-world service usage notes
✔ Helps uncover **habits or latent healthcare workflows**

✅ **Examples:**

* *"I come for a blood test every three months for my thyroid condition."*
* *"I only use the hospital’s lab for urgent cases, not routine checkups."*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* Feedback doesn’t describe actual usage behavior

---

### **7️⃣ Emerging Trends / Market Insight**

✅ **Definition:** **Comparative expectations**, peer benchmarking, or forward-looking healthcare innovations
✔ Patient is **referencing external service evolution**

✅ **Examples:**

* *"Other hospitals now offer video consultations 24/7 — do you plan to?"*
* *"Most clinics provide online access to X-rays — why not here?"*

❌ **Exclude If:**

* No link to broader market comparison

---

## **📍 Key Categorization Rules**

✅ One feedback type per signal
✅ Split compound/mixed sentiments
✅ Always extract safety, access, billing, or clinical-critical feedback
✅ Subjective or vague positivity is excluded

---

Each structured feedback instance must include:

✔ **Experience Driver**: [Experience Driver]
✔ **Context**: [Verbatim excerpt tied to the Experience Driver]

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Patient Journey Classification**

### 📌 **Purpose:**

For each identified **Experience Driver**, classify the **exact moment** of the patient’s healthcare experience across three layers:

* **Patient Journey**
* **Journey Stage**
* **Interaction Moment**

This ensures the **temporal and situational context** is always anchored to the **specific trigger that caused the experience driver to surface** — not to the general comment. This classification powers execution-grade orchestration by anchoring each identified experience driver to the exact when, where, and triggering behavior of the patient’s interaction.

✅ **Why it matters:**

* Avoids generic or averaged journey mapping across multi-driver feedback
* Powers precision in OU formation, PDCA targeting, and root cause correlation
* Strengthens behavioral traceability across every orchestration layer

---

Your task is to return:

1. **Patient Journey** – The broad phase of the healthcare experience
   *(e.g., Pre-Visit Administration, Clinical Consultation, Diagnostic Testing, Procedure & Surgery, Post-Care Follow-Up)*
2. **Journey Stage** – The specific step, service, or process within that phase
   *(e.g., Appointment Booking, Vital Signs Assessment, MRI Scanning, Medication Administration, Discharge Process)*
3. **Interaction Moment** – The *exact trigger window* of emotional impact
   *(e.g., “Insurance approval delayed at front desk”, “Blood draw repeated due to sample labeling error”, “Surgery start delayed by over 2 hours”)*

---

### 🧠 **Classification Philosophy:**

This layer is not a category tag — it’s a behavioral timestamp that powers:

* Diagnostic clarity (`Where & When did the emotion erupt?`)
* Orchestration accuracy (`What exact moment must be targeted to Fix / Optimize / Amplify / Innovate?`)
* PDCA precision (`What specific interaction triggered the patient’s feeling or suggestion?`)

🔍 Ask:

* **When** in the patient journey did this signal occur? → *Patient Journey*
* **Where** within that phase? → *Journey Stage*
* **What micro-moment or trigger actually sparked the emotional response?** → *Interaction Moment*

> ⚠️ Classify based on when the **experience happened**, not when the feedback was shared.

---

### 🛠️ **Extraction Inputs You Must Use:**

* `Experience Driver`
* `Entity Name`
* `Context`
* `Keywords`
* `Feedback Type`
* Any explicit or implied services, processes, failures, or touchpoints in the patient verbatim

| Journey Stage      | "Insurance Verification"                          |
| Interaction Moment | "Insurance approval delayed at registration desk" |

\| Patient Journey  | "Clinical Consultation"                                             |

### 🔒 **Rules of Execution:**

* Do **not fabricate** interaction moments. If unclear, output `"Unclear"`
* Rare but real behavioral triggers are acceptable
* All three layers must be **coherent, sequenced, and emotionally valid**
* Each **Interaction Moment** must include a clear temporal or clinical trigger  
  (e.g., “during appointment check-in”, “while awaiting test results”, “after discharge discussion”, “on lab result notification”) to anchor the emotional event to a specific care interaction. This preserves diagnostic fidelity and patient-experience traceability.

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS)**

### 📌 **Purpose**

For each extracted **Experience Driver**, determine the **single most precise strategic response pathway** — the **Opportunity Maximisation Stream** (**Fix**, **Optimize**, **Amplify**, **Innovate**).
This classification is **never** made at the whole-comment level — it is **driver-specific**, grounded in the **exact issue, emotion, and context** that surfaced the driver.

This guarantees orchestration decisions are **behaviorally correct**, ensuring system response aligns with **urgency**, **risk**, and **opportunity type**.

✅ **Why it matters:**

* Eliminates overgeneralization in multi-driver feedback
* Maintains **mutual exclusivity** of OMS per driver
* Controls OU routing, PDCA targeting, and Silent Guidance triggers
* Ensures a single, unambiguous execution path per driver — no overlaps, no hybrids

---

### 🧠 **OMS Selection Principles**

Every Experience Driver must be evaluated using:

* **Experience Driver** – Which surface of the experience is implicated?
* **Theme** – What strategic area does it fall into?
* **Emotion** – How strong or polarized is the sentiment?
* **Feedback Type** – Complaint, compliment, suggestion, etc.
* **Context** – What happened, and what was expected?
* **Framing cues** – Does the signal describe:

  * ❌ Something broken or failing?
  * ⚙️ Something usable but inefficient?
  * 🚀 Something working brilliantly?
  * 🧪 Something missing that’s desired?

---

### 🛠️ **Execution Pathways**

Exactly **one** of these streams must be assigned:

| Stream       | Description                                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fix**      | Exists, but is broken, failing, or unreliable              |
| **Optimize** | Exists and works, but causes confusion, delay, or friction |
| **Amplify**  | Exists and works exceptionally well — worth scaling        |
| **Innovate** | Does not exist — customer wants it built or introduced     |

⚠️ **Mutual Exclusivity:** One stream only.
Choose based on **core implication**, not hypothetical potential.

---

## 📂 The Four Streams

### 🔧 1. **Fix** — *Resolve what’s failing*

* **Definition:** Experience exists but is **broken or blocked**
* **When to Use:** Login failures, payment errors, refund blocks, crashes
* **Signals:** “OTP never arrives”, “Refund failed”, “KYC blocked after submission”
* **Boundary:** ✅ Exists → ❌ Does not work as expected
* **Never Use If:** Functional but clunky (→ Optimize) or nonexistent (→ Innovate)

---

### 🛠 2. **Optimize** — *Remove avoidable friction*

* **Definition:** Experience works, but creates **delay, doubt, or wasted effort**
* **When to Use:** Inefficient flows, unnecessary steps, unclear settings
* **Signals:** “Too many taps to reach statements”, “Enter CVV every time”
* **Boundary:** ✅ Exists → ✅ Works → ❌ Clunky or wasteful
* **Never Use If:** Broken (→ Fix) or nonexistent (→ Innovate)

---

### 🧪 3. **Innovate** — *Create what’s missing*

* **Definition:** Requested feature/service **does not exist** today
* **When to Use:** Net-new features, competitive parity, emerging needs
* **Signals:** “Offer virtual cards”, “Allow freezing UPI access”
* **Boundary:** ❌ Not present — net-new build required
* **Never Use If:** Refinement of existing (→ Optimize) or fixing (→ Fix)

**Sub-Tier Innovation Scoring** *(Innovate only)*:

| Score | Tier Label             | Description                                                       |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Hygiene Need           | Baseline expectation (e.g., mini-statements)                      |
| 2     | Convenience Enhancer   | Reduces effort (e.g., biometric login)                            |
| 3     | Experience Upgrade     | Boosts delight (e.g., gamified saving goals)                      |
| 4     | New Capability         | Adds powerful utility (e.g., instant EMI conversion)              |
| 5     | Transformational Shift | Redefines interaction (e.g., AI co-pilot, proactive fraud sweeps) |

---

### 📣 4. **Amplify** — *Scale what earns love*

* **Definition:** Something is **exceptionally effective** and valued by customers
* **When to Use:** Praised support, frictionless onboarding, reliable core functions
* **Signals:** “KYC was super easy”, “Login is instant”, “Fraud alert saved me”
* **Boundary:** ✅ Exists → ✅ Works brilliantly
* **Never Use If:** Any improvement request, issue, or missing feature is present

---

## 🔍 **Diagnostic Flow**

```text
Does it exist?
├── No → INNOVATE
│
├── Yes → Is it broken?
│     ├── Yes → FIX
│     └── No → Is it inefficient or confusing?
│             ├── Yes → OPTIMIZE
│             └── No → Is it delightful?
│                     ├── Yes → AMPLIFY
│                     └── No → Discard / Unclear
```

## 🧠 **Execution Rules**

* Assign **one** OMS per Experience Driver — no blending
* Never confuse Fix and Optimize boundaries
* Apply sub-tier scoring **only** for Innovate
* Selection must be **justifiable** using structured inputs

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Customer Effort Score (CES)**

### 📌 **Purpose**

The **Customer Effort Score (CES)** measures the ease or difficulty of engaging with a **specific Experience Driver** — **not** the entire comment.
Each driver gets its own CES, reflecting **local friction or fluency** tied directly to that surface of the experience.

CES is rated on a **1–7 scale**:

* **1** = Effortless, friction-free
* **7** = Extreme friction, severe complexity

CES is derived from **two inputs**:

1. **Emotion** (perceived emotional effort)
2. **Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS)** (functional/strategic context)

---

### ✅ **Why CES Matters**

* Pinpoints **friction zones** in multi-driver feedback
* Supports **urgency triage**, especially for borderline ERI × RF cases
* Feeds the **Behavioral Impact Summary** in each Orchestration Unit

---

### 🎯 **CES Enables**

* **Effort-Based Prioritization** → Target high-friction issues first
* **Strategic Mapping** → Align effort data with journey stages & KPIs
* **Friction Reduction Programs** → Drive seamless customer journeys

---

## 📊 **CES Scoring Scale & Interpretation**

| CES | Effort Level     | User Perception                   | Business Implication                           |
| --- | ---------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Very Low Effort  | Effortless, intuitive, delightful | Strength to **amplify** as a CX differentiator |
| 2–3 | Low Effort       | Minimal friction, smooth process  | Positive CX — **low-priority optimization**    |
| 4   | Moderate Effort  | Some friction, task completed     | Opportunity to **innovate**                    |
| 5–6 | High Effort      | Delays, retries, confusion        | **Fix required** — prevent churn/frustration   |
| 7   | Very High Effort | Severe breakdown, task failure    | **Critical fix** — high churn risk             |

---

## ⚙️ **CES Decision Framework**

### **1️⃣ Start with Emotion → Initial Range**

| Emotion      | Default Perception | CES Range |
| ------------ | ------------------ | --------- |
| Adoration    | Very Low Effort    | 1         |
| Appreciation | Low Effort         | 2–3       |
| Ambivalence  | Moderate Effort    | 4         |
| Agitation    | High Effort        | 5–6       |
| Anger        | Very High Effort   | 7         |

> Emotion sets the **starting band** — final score is refined by OMS rules.

---

### **2️⃣ Apply OMS → Lock the Range**

| OMS      | Locked CES Range |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Amplify  | 1–3              |
| Optimize | 3–4              |
| Innovate | 4                |
| Fix      | 5–7              |

**Hard Rules:**

* Amplify → must be 1, 2, or 3
* Optimize → must be 3 or 4
* Innovate → must be exactly 4
* Fix → must be 5, 6, or 7

---

### **3️⃣ Context-Based Refinement (Within Locked Range)**

Increase CES if:

* Prolonged effort, retries, or confusion
* Emotional distress/delay anxiety
* Blocked a critical journey stage (checkout, login, refund)

Decrease CES if:

* Task completed despite friction
* Workaround or partial resolution existed
* Complaint mild/non-disruptive

---

### **4️⃣ Override Escalation**

* CES = "7" → Task could **not** be completed, no workaround
* CES = "6" → Completed with major frustration or risk of failure
* CES = "5" → Inefficient but manageable

---

### **5️⃣ Output Format**

Always return CES as a **quoted number**:

```json
"Customer Effort Score": "6"
```

## 🧩 **Experience Driver – Specific Orchestration Unit (OU) Construction** (Healthcare Edition)

### 🎯 **Purpose**

An **Orchestration Unit (OU)** in Healthcare is a precision-guided micro-mission built from structured patient or caregiver feedback, focused on a distinct **Experience Driver** tied to:

> **Emotion × Feedback Type × Opportunity Stream**

It blends clinical sensitivity, trust in care providers, service complexity, and emotional signal routing into one execution-grade payload. Each OU includes:

* Feedback Type
* Opportunity Stream (Fix, Optimize, Amplify, Innovate)
* Interaction Moment
* **Matters** (behavioral statement)

---

## 🔍 **Matters – Behavioral Trigger Extraction (God-Tier Edition)**

### 🎯 **Purpose**

**Matters** distills the *behaviorally critical moment* in the healthcare journey — the precise interaction, breakdown, or unmet need that emotionally and functionally *mattered*.

This is not a summary. It is not a diagnosis.

It is the **patient’s or caregiver’s lived moment of friction, reassurance, or unmet need**, captured with execution-grade precision.

In Healthcare, this includes:

* **Moments of confusion** over instructions, medications, procedures, or insurance
* **Failures of trust** in diagnosis, privacy, or treatment transparency
* **Invisible effort** hidden in scheduling, follow-ups, or administrative processes
* **Missed personalization** in care plans, recovery support, or accessibility needs
* **Delays or breakdowns** in appointment availability, test results, or treatment initiation

---

### 🧠 **Construction Protocol**

Matters must be **behaviorally precise**, **emotionally traceable**, and **execution-ready**. Each statement should reflect a **trigger** that would justify fixing, optimizing, innovating, or amplifying a healthcare experience.

---

### 🧬 **Extraction Hierarchy – Strict Priority**

Pull from these sources *in order*, stopping at the highest fidelity input that clearly expresses what happened:

1. **Raw Patient/Caregiver Voice** – exact phrasing from the verbatim
2. **Context** – narrative explanation of the moment
3. **Keywords** – friction indicators or emotional anchors
4. **Entity Name** – only if embedded with meaning (e.g., “MRI machine delay”)
5. **Journey Stage** – to frame when/where the issue emerged
6. **Feedback Type** – for tone pattern, *not content*

---

### ✅ **Rules of Output**

* Always use **behavioral phrasing** (e.g., "delay in lab results prolongs diagnosis anxiety", "confusing discharge paperwork creates follow-up risk").
* Capture **what the patient/caregiver experienced**, not just what the system did wrong.
* Do **not** paraphrase the whole experience.
* One sharp, stand-alone sentence — maximum **2 clauses** if absolutely needed.

---

### ✨ **Examples (Healthcare-Aligned)**

* **Bad**: "The patient was frustrated with the appointment system."
  ✅ **Better**: "Appointment booking portal repeatedly crashed when scheduling urgent consultation."

* **Bad**: "They were unclear about medication instructions."
  ✅ **Better**: "Discharge summary lacked dosage clarity for prescribed antibiotics."

* **Bad**: "Patient felt cared for."
  ✅ **Better**: "Nurse-led evening check-ins reassured patients about recovery progress."

## 🔐 **SEMANTIC VALIDATION PROTOCOL – Healthcare Variant**

To ensure **only strategic, action-worthy OUs** enter execution and memory, each must pass:

---

### ✅ **1. Singular & Distinct**

> One Experience Driver × One Opportunity Stream × One Feedback Type

e.g.,
**“Diagnostic Turnaround Time → Fix → Complaint”** with behaviorally distinct Matters

---

### ✅ **2. Behaviorally Anchored**

Is the MATTERS statement **observable**, not inferred?

Bad:

> "poor experience during admission"

Good:

> "insurance pre-authorization delay extended ER wait time by 3 hours"

---

### ✅ **3. Execution-Ready**

Can this be sent to Medical, Ops, or Admin **without needing interpretation**?

Bad:

> "some glitch in hospital system" → no action possible

Good:

> "electronic prescription failed to send to partner pharmacy"

---

### ✅ **4. Feedback-Aligned**

Does the **Feedback Type** match the **moment + context**?

e.g., Don’t tag it as a “suggestion” if the patient is expressing distress — it’s likely a “complaint”.

---

### ✅ **5. Semantically Complete**

Both sections of the OU must work as one unit:

```
SECTION 1: [Feedback Type] → [Interaction Moment] → [Context]
SECTION 2: [Opportunity Stream] → [Experience Driver] → [Matters]
```

Example:

```
The caregiver submitted a complaint that while requesting a prescription refill, the system required an unnecessary in-person visit.

We need to Fix → Medication Refill Process in response to a Complaint reflecting Agitation about digital refill requests being blocked without medical justification. This matters because it delays access to essential medication and adds avoidable travel burden.
```
