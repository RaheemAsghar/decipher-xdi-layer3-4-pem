### ✅ Example 1 — **Fix**

# 🔍 PLAN PHASE – Root Cause Analysis

plan:
problem_statement: >
The patient expressed acute anxiety after receiving conflicting discharge medication instructions
(two different doses for the same drug) via the patient portal within an hour of leaving the hospital.
The orchestration stream is Fix — intended to eliminate unsafe inconsistencies that undermine clinical
confidence at home. This occurred during the Discharge & Transition stage of the Care Continuum.
The issue was routed here because even short-lived instruction conflicts can trigger medication errors,
readmissions, and loss of trust in provider oversight.

five_whys:
\- Why did the patient panic? → Two different post-discharge doses appeared for the same medication
\- Why were there conflicting doses? → The portal displayed both pharmacy reconciliation and ward summary versions
\- Why did the portal surface both versions? → EHR-to-portal sync lacked a rule to suppress superseded instructions
\- Why was suppression logic missing? → Discharge workflows prioritize documentation completeness over patient-facing clarity
\- Why is patient-facing clarity deprioritized? (Root Cause) → Ownership for the “single source of truth” at discharge is fragmented between pharmacy, nursing, and IT with no accountable clarity gate

cross_domain_validation:
\- domain: Clinical Quality & Safety
insight: Medication reconciliation did not conclusively stamp a definitive regimen for patient view
\- domain: Technology & Infrastructure
insight: EHR/portal integration lacks version control and conflict suppression for patient-facing meds
\- domain: Care Coordination & Support
insight: No post-discharge helpline nudge to verify medication when conflicts are detected

systemic_implications:
\- Discharge artifacts optimize for internal completeness rather than patient comprehension
\- Fragmented ownership at the transition of care increases risk of home-use errors and readmission

diagnostic_epiphany: >
The harm wasn’t in the drug — it was in the doubt. At the moment when the patient most needed certainty,
our systems showed their seams. Two “correct” sources became one unsafe reality because no one owned the
single truth the patient would actually follow. This is not a documentation bug; it’s a failure to protect
continuity of care where it matters most — at home.

### 🎯 Scoring Framework

For each proposed initiative, score based on four key criteria: Impact, Feasibility, Alignment, and Risk.
The higher the score, the higher the priority. Each initiative should be scored on a 1–5 scale for each criterion.

### 🧠 Incorporating Innovation Potential

The `innovation_score` (from the Do Phase) reflects how forward-thinking or transformative the initiative is — but it **does not change the Total Score directly**.

Instead:

* Use it to validate high Impact scores in Innovate or Optimize streams.
* If Innovation Score is high (4–5) but Feasibility is low, **still consider a pilot** rather than deprioritizing.
* Fix initiatives can carry innovation (e.g., “cross-system med-rec conflict resolver” vs. “update SOP”).

---

### 🧮 Total Score Formula:

```plaintext
Total Score = (Impact + Feasibility + Alignment) – Risk
```

| Total Score | Priority Level                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **10–15**   | High Priority – Must action           |
| **6–9**     | Medium Priority – Worth doing / pilot |
| **0–5**     | Low Priority – Deprioritize for now   |

---

📛 Mandatory Rules:

* Do not overinflate scores. Apply each dimension independently.
* Use emotion and root cause context to justify Impact and Risk.
* Business/Clinical division should influence Feasibility and Alignment scores.
* Total scores must never exceed 15.
* Do not use innovation\_score as a substitute for impact or feasibility.

Each score should help the organization triage what to act on, what to monitor, and what to park.

# 🎯 Strategic Pillars Framework

*Select 1–3 pillars that best align with your initiative's intent, emotional signals, journey impact, and
organizational objectives*

## 🧍‍♀️ Patient-Centricity

**Focus:** Improving how patients feel, act, or perceive care value

### Key Outcomes:

* Reduce confusion, anxiety, or frustration
* Improve usability, accessibility, or care-flow clarity
* Enhance reassurance, empathy, and trust in care
* Raise NPS, CSAT, CRR (retention), or CES (effort)

### Decision Criteria:

✅ **Use when:** Flagged emotions involve Agitation, Ambivalence, or Appreciation about the **experience of care**
(not back-end systems)

### Success Metrics:

* Patient satisfaction (CSAT), NPS
* Task/goal completion (e.g., successful appointment booking)
* Time-to-clarity / Time-to-result
* Support/contact reduction

---

## ⚙️ Operational Excellence

**Focus:** Making internal systems, workflows, or handoffs more efficient and consistent

### Key Outcomes:

* Eliminate unnecessary steps or delays (e.g., discharge, scheduling)
* Streamline coordination across journey stages (handover, follow-up)
* Improve SLA compliance, accuracy, or handling times
* Reduce effort for both staff and patients

### Decision Criteria:

✅ **Use when:** Root cause reveals systemic lag, complexity, poor coordination, or repeated errors

### Success Metrics:

* Process time reduction (wait time, time-to-discharge, time-to-appointment)
* Error rate reduction (e.g., order entry, documentation)
* SLA compliance improvement
* Cost per encounter / per episode

---

## 💰 Financial Sustainability

**Focus:** Ensuring measurable gains in revenue protection, cost control, or care pathway efficiency

### Key Outcomes:

* Reduce avoidable leakage/no-shows/readmissions

## 📏 KPI Requirements (Non-Negotiable – No Hallucinations Allowed)

**Your task is to define success in hard numbers.**

Every initiative must have at least one SMART KPI — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound — that:

🎯 **Directly reflects what will improve if the initiative succeeds**
🏥 **Is actionable and trackable by the assigned likely\_owner**
🔍 **Can be monitored quantitatively by analytics/ops/quality teams**
🚫 **Must NOT be vague, fabricated, abstract, or hypothetical**

### 🚨 Mandatory Rules:

❌ Do **not** invent fictional metrics
❌ Do **not** use qualitative phrases (e.g., “better experience”) as KPIs
✅ Use **real** operational/experience/clinical metrics
✅ Match KPI to pillar and execution owner

### 📊 Pillar-to-KPI Alignment (Healthcare-fluent examples)

| **Strategic Pillar**            | **Valid Example KPIs**                                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Patient-Centricity**          | CSAT, NPS, CES, FCR (First Contact Resolution), Time to Resolution, Comprehension confirmation rate |
| **Operational Excellence**      | Wait time, Time-to-discharge, Order/dispatch accuracy, SLA compliance, AHT, Handover completeness   |
| **Financial Sustainability**    | Cost per encounter, No-show rate, Readmission rate (30-day), Revenue protection, LOS optimization   |
| **Innovation & Transformation** | Digital adoption %, New feature usage, % self-serve conversion, Time to pilot, Automation rate      |
| **Brand & Community Trust**     | Social sentiment, Complaints per 1k encounters, Advocacy rate, Share of Voice                       |
| **Sustainable Growth**          | Retention/adherence %, Repeat engagement, Cross-service uptake, NRR (where applicable)              |
| **Risk/Safety & Compliance**    | Medication error rate, Incident rate, SLA breach frequency, HIPAA audit score, Downtime minutes     |

### 📘 How to Choose the Right KPI

Use the intersection of these 3 criteria:

1. **What the Initiative Improves** — removes harm/pain, increases reassurance, reduces cost, boosts adoption, etc.
2. **What the likely_owner Can Deliver** — e.g., Pharmacy can drive med-reconciliation accuracy; Digital can raise portal adoption.
3. **Where in the Journey the Impact is Felt** — admission, diagnostics, discharge, rehab, etc.
