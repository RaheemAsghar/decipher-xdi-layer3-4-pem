🧠 SECTION 3: Experience Driver – Specific Context
(Healthcare Edition — DecipherOS)

📌 Purpose

The Experience Driver Context defines **why a specific Experience Driver exists**.

It isolates the **exact patient-perceived trigger, failure, friction, success,
or opportunity** that caused the Experience Driver to surface — independent of
all other signals in the same feedback record.

Context is not narrative.
Context is not summary.
Context is not emotion.

Context is the **behavioral explanation** that makes the Experience Driver
auditable, actionable, and executable.

Each Experience Driver must be contextualized **in isolation**, even when
multiple drivers originate from the same review, case note, or interaction.

This context becomes a **foundational execution input** for:

• Opportunity Stream routing  
• Risk and impact assessment  
• Orchestration Unit (OU) formation  
• PDCA planning and outcome evaluation  

⚠️ Never generalize across the full feedback record.
Context must bind **exclusively** to the specific Experience Driver under
evaluation.

---

🧭 Context Principles (Locked)

• Context must describe a **patient-impacting moment**
• Context must reflect **apparent failure, friction, success, or opportunity**
• Context must remain **within the organization’s domain of responsibility**
• Context must be **independently actionable**
• Vague, ambient, or non-causal language is invalid

---

## 📍 Context Extraction Rules

### 1️⃣ Responsibility-Bound Specificity

Extract context only when the feedback **explicitly links** the Experience Driver
to a concrete patient-impacting event, delay, defect, incident, or success.

The context must explain **what happened**, **where it manifested**, or
**what obligation was unmet or fulfilled**.

🟢 Valid Context:
• “Appointment was cancelled on the same day without notification.”
• “Lab results were uploaded two days late, delaying treatment.”
• “Telehealth session disconnected twice during consultation.”

🔴 Invalid Context:
• “The hospital is fine.”
• “I like the clinic building.”
• “Healthcare is slow.”

---

### 2️⃣ Keyword Anchoring (Evidence Binding)

Extract keywords that **anchor the context to a real operational, clinical,
or experiential surface**.

Keywords are not sentiment descriptors.
Keywords expose **what failed, worked, or was constrained**.

🧩 Examples:

| Context                                                   | Keywords                                     |
|-----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| “MRI results were misplaced; scan had to be repeated.”    | Lost records, duplicate scan, admin error    |
| “Doctor explained diagnosis clearly and patiently.”       | Patient education, explanation quality       |
| “Booking system crashed during appointment scheduling.”   | System outage, booking failure               |

Keywords must reinforce **actionability**, not emotion.

---

### 3️⃣ Actionable Depth Requirement

Context must contain sufficient clarity to support **investigation,
correction, amplification, or improvement**.

Pure emotional expression without a causal anchor is invalid.

🟢 Include:
• “Medication was out of stock and no alternative was offered.”
• “Insurance claim denied without explanation.”

🔴 Exclude:
• “Hospitals are frustrating.”
• “This clinic is not for me.”

---

### 4️⃣ Multi-Outcome Context Handling

If a **single Experience Driver** produces **multiple distinct outcomes**,
each outcome must be captured as a **separate contextual instance**.

Never collapse distinct consequences into a single context.

🧩 Example:

Feedback:
“The doctor was very attentive, but the billing process took over an hour.”

Extract:
• Context A: “Doctor was attentive and explained treatment clearly.”
• Context B: “Billing process caused excessive delay.”

---

### 5️⃣ Business-Critical Priority Override

Always extract context that surfaces:

• Patient safety risks or adverse events  
• Clinical or administrative errors  
• Consent, privacy, or regulatory breaches  
• Access barriers affecting care delivery  

These contexts override precision thresholds and must be captured even if
language is imprecise or emotionally neutral.

🟢 Mandatory Extraction:
• “Wrong patient file shown during consultation.”
• “Clinic not wheelchair accessible.”
• “Medical records shared without consent.”

---

### 6️⃣ Non-Actionable Context Exclusion

If an Experience Driver is valid but the context adds **no causal or actionable
information**, the instance must be discarded.

🟢 Valid:
• “Surgery date was changed without informing me.”
• “Discharge summary listed incorrect medication dosage.”

🔴 Invalid:
• “The hospital is decent.”
• “No issues so far.”

---

## 📦 Required Context Payload (Per Driver)

Each extracted Experience Driver must include:

• Experience Driver — canonical Category → Subcategory  
• Context — concise, responsibility-bound explanation  
• Keywords — operational anchors tied to the context  

Context enriches execution.
It never alters identity.

---

## 🧠 Anti-Ambiguity Enforcement

• Do not infer causes not stated by the patient
• Do not normalize, soften, or reinterpret failures
• Do not generalize across episodes
• Do not invent precision

If timing, interaction moment, or process step is unclear:
→ record as “Unspecified”

---

## 🧠 Enforcement Statement

This layer is an **execution integrity boundary**.

No Orchestration Unit, PDCA plan, or outcome evaluation may be generated
from an Experience Driver lacking clear, responsibility-bound context.

Context exists to make Experience Drivers **executable truth**, not narrative.

---

🧠 SECTION 4: Experience Driver – Feedback Type
(Healthcare Edition — DecipherOS)

📌 Purpose

Feedback Type classifies the **communicative intent** of a specific
Experience Driver.

It answers one question only:

“What class of organizational response is the patient invoking
through this signal?”

Feedback Type is applied **per Experience Driver**, never per review.

It does not measure emotion.
It does not describe severity.
It does not imply priority.

It determines **how the system is allowed to respond**.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• One Experience Driver → one Feedback Type
• Feedback Type reflects **intent**, not tone
• Classification is based on **what the patient is asking the organization to do**
• Safety, access, failure, and unmet responsibility always dominate
• Ambiguous cases must resolve toward **organizational obligation**, not generosity

If multiple intents exist, the signal must be **split**.

---

## 🔐 Canonical Feedback Type Set (Closed)

Each Experience Driver must be classified into **exactly one** of the following
seven categories.

No additions. No hybrids.

---

### 1️⃣ Complaint  *(Negative signal requiring correction)*

✅ Definition

A Complaint is an expression of **apparent failure, unmet obligation, defect,
breakdown, delay, error, omission, or adverse incident** within the healthcare
organization’s **domain of responsibility**, where the organization is expected
to **investigate, explain, correct, mitigate, or resolve**.

A Complaint always implies:
• something that should have worked but did not
• or something that should have happened but did not
• or something unsafe, incorrect, or obstructive occurred

A Complaint **always routes to Fix**.

---

🧩 Internal Complaint Signal Classes (Non-Optional)

Complaints encompass **three mandatory internal categories**:

1. **Service Quality Failures**
   Breakdowns in communication, responsiveness, coordination, follow-through,
   staff conduct, or care continuity.

2. **Functionality / Process Defects**
   Broken systems, failed workflows, scheduling breakdowns, access barriers,
   documentation errors, digital or administrative failures.

3. **Care, Safety, or Rights Incidents**
   Clinical errors, missed follow-ups, medication issues, diagnostic delays,
   privacy violations, consent breaches, or safety risks — regardless of harm.

---

🛑 Mandatory Complaint Extraction

Always classify as Complaint when feedback surfaces:

• delays contradicting expectations or commitments  
• access denial or excessive waiting  
• system or portal failures  
• scheduling or cancellation failures  
• test result delays or missing follow-ups  
• billing or insurance processing errors  
• medication or care coordination issues  
• safety concerns or adverse incidents  
• administrative or clinical process breakdowns  

Tone does not matter.
Politeness does not matter.
Severity does not matter.

If it represents **apparent failure**, it is a Complaint.

---

🟢 Complaint Examples

Service Quality:
• “No one updated me while I waited for hours.”
• “I kept calling but never got a response.”

Functionality / Process:
• “The appointment system keeps crashing.”
• “My lab test was scheduled but no one came.”

Care / Safety:
• “My medication dosage was entered incorrectly.”
• “I was discharged without follow-up instructions.”

---

🔴 Exclude as Complaint Only If:

• No identifiable experience determinant exists
• Pure emotion without a surface (“I hate this place”)
• Generic condemnation without causality

---

### 2️⃣ Compliment  *(Positive reinforcement)*

✅ Definition

Explicit praise for a **specific**, patient-visible service, interaction,
process, or capability that worked well.

Compliments reinforce what should be **protected or amplified**.

---

🟢 Include:
• “The nurse explained my post-surgery care clearly.”
• “The lab technician was calm and reassuring.”

🔴 Exclude:
• “Great hospital.”
• “I love this place.”

---

### 3️⃣ Request  *(Expectation of provision or enablement)*

✅ Definition

An explicit expectation for **core capability, access, support, or provision**
that the patient believes **should exist** to enable care, safety, or continuity.

Requests reflect **missing but required functionality**.

Requests often imply urgency or entitlement.

Requests may route to Fix or Innovate depending on capability existence.

---

🟢 Include:
• “There should be an emergency after-hours hotline.”
• “Please allow online prescription renewal.”

🔴 Exclude:
• Clearly optional enhancements → Suggestion

---

### 4️⃣ Suggestion  *(Optional improvement idea)*

✅ Definition

A **non-critical**, optional enhancement that improves comfort, convenience,
or experience quality **without affecting correctness, safety, or access**.

Suggestions never imply failure.

---

🟢 Include:
• “It would be nice to have quieter waiting rooms.”
• “Add digital check-in kiosks.”

🔴 Exclude:
• Anything implying obligation, urgency, or safety → Request or Complaint

---

### 5️⃣ Question  *(Clarification or information need)*

✅ Definition

A genuine request for explanation, status, policy, eligibility, or process
clarity that is **answerable**.

Questions do not assert failure unless failure is explicitly stated.

---

🟢 Include:
• “Can I get my lab reports emailed?”
• “Is the flu vaccine available for children?”

🔴 Exclude:
• Rhetorical accusations (“Why do you never answer calls?”)

---

### 6️⃣ Product Usage Insight  *(Behavioral pattern observation)*

✅ Definition

Description of **how the patient actually uses**, adapts, sequences, or works
around a service, product, or process.

Usage Insights reveal latent workflows, habits, or friction patterns.

---

🟢 Include:
• “I come every three months for blood tests.”
• “I only use telehealth for urgent cases.”

🔴 Exclude:
• Opinion without behavioral description

---

### 7️⃣ Emerging Trends / Market Insight  *(External comparison signal)*

✅ Definition

Comparative or forward-looking signal referencing **external benchmarks,
industry evolution, or peer expectations** beyond the individual case.

---

🟢 Include:
• “Other hospitals offer 24/7 video consults.”
• “Most clinics provide online access to X-rays.”

🔴 Exclude:
• Pure internal complaints without comparison

---

## 🔍 Classification Enforcement Rules

• One Feedback Type per Experience Driver
• Split compound signals
• Safety, access, billing, and care failures always dominate
• If in doubt between Complaint vs Suggestion → Complaint
• If in doubt between Request vs Suggestion → Request

---

## 📦 Required Output Fields

Each Experience Driver instance must include:

• Experience Driver  
• Feedback Type  
• Context (verbatim excerpt tied to the driver)

---

🧠 Enforcement Statement

Feedback Type is a **response-governance boundary**.

Incorrect classification corrupts:
• opportunity stream routing
• Fix vs Optimize separation
• downstream orchestration integrity

No Experience Driver may proceed without a **single, defensible Feedback Type**.

🧠 SECTION 5: Experience Driver – Specific Emotion
(Healthcare Edition — DecipherOS)

📌 Purpose

The Emotion dimension captures the **dominant emotional response** associated
with each extracted Experience Driver.

Emotion must be bound **to the specific driver**, not to the overall patient
comment.

Emotion functions as a **weighting and interpretation signal** for downstream
deterministic computation (e.g., ERI, prioritization posture, escalation tone).

Emotion does NOT:
• determine Feedback Type
• determine the opportunity stream
• change Experience Driver identity
• imply clinical severity or medical risk

Feedback Type governs response class.
Emotion governs emotional weight.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• Emotion is assigned **per Experience Driver**, never per review  
• One emotion per driver (most behaviorally meaningful)  
• Emotion must be grounded in patient language, tone, and context  
• No imaginative inference, no clinical interpretation, no escalation  
• If unclear or emotionally flat → default to Ambivalence with low confidence  

⚠️ Boundary Reminder:
This layer captures **patient-expressed emotional reality**, not medical,
clinical, or institutional judgment.

## 🔎 Emotion Assignment Logic (Deterministic)

1️⃣ **Context-First**
Determine emotion from the lived experience described — not keyword spotting.

2️⃣ **Driver-Bound**
The emotion must apply to the specific Experience Driver being emitted.

3️⃣ **Behavioral Intensity**
Select the emotion that best reflects behavioral force
(e.g., “Frustrated” over “Uneasy” when justified).

4️⃣ **Single Assignment Rule**
Exactly one emotion (Group + Specific Feeling) per driver.

5️⃣ **Uncertainty Handling**
If emotion cannot be confidently determined:
• Group: Ambivalence  
• Feeling: Indifferent  
• Confidence: Low  

6️⃣ **Sarcasm Handling**
Classify by intent, not wording:
“Thanks for nothing” → Anger – Resentful

---

## 🧷 Mixed Emotion Resolution

If multiple emotional cues exist **for the same driver**, enforce a single-label
selection using this hierarchy:

Anger → Agitation → Ambivalence → Appreciation → Adoration

🧠 SECTION 7: Experience Driver – Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS)
(Healthcare Edition — DecipherOS)

📌 Purpose

For each extracted Experience Driver, assign **one and only one**
Opportunity Maximisation Stream (OMS):

• Fix
• Optimize
• Amplify
• Innovate

OMS defines the **single authoritative execution pathway** the system is
permitted to take in response to the Experience Driver.

OMS is:
• driver-specific (never review-level)
• mutually exclusive
• grounded in the patient’s stated experience
• enforced before OU formation and PDCA planning

OMS selection determines **what kind of action is allowed**, not how it is executed.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• One Experience Driver → one OMS
• OMS is determined by **patient-declared reality**, not inferred risk
• Feedback Type constrains OMS eligibility
• Emotion does not determine OMS
• Apparent failure always dominates friction

If multiple interpretations seem possible, resolve toward
**organizational obligation**, not optimism.

---

## 🔍 OMS Determination Inputs (Mandatory)

OMS must be determined using only:

• Experience Driver (identity)
• Context (what happened)
• Feedback Type (intent class)
• Interaction Moment (trigger window)
• Explicit patient language

You must NOT use:
• inferred clinical severity
• regulatory sensitivity
• institutional anxiety
• hypothetical downstream harm

---

## 🛠️ Canonical OMS Set (Closed)

Exactly one stream must be selected.

| Stream       | Core Meaning                                               |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Fix          | Exists but failed, broke, was unsafe, or unmet responsibility |
| Optimize     | Exists and worked, but caused avoidable effort or friction |
| Amplify     | Exists and worked exceptionally well                       |
| Innovate     | Does not exist; patient desires it to exist                |

---

## 🔧 1️⃣ FIX — Resolve Apparent Failure or Unmet Responsibility

### Definition

Assign **Fix** when the experience **existed** but:

• failed to occur as expected  
• was incorrect, unsafe, unreliable, or incomplete  
• involved non-response or omission  
• breached reasonable patient expectations of duty of care  

Fix applies **regardless of root cause, intent, or attribution**.

If the patient indicates that something **should have happened and did not**,
the correct stream is Fix.

---

### Mandatory Fix Triggers

Always assign Fix when feedback includes:

• missed, delayed, or incorrect care delivery  
• non-response to patient outreach or alerts  
• appointment failures or unexplained cancellations  
• test results lost, delayed, or not communicated  
• medication errors or unclear instructions  
• diagnostic or treatment delays  
• patient safety concerns or adverse incidents  
• administrative or clinical process breakdowns  

Tone is irrelevant.
Severity is irrelevant.
Politeness is irrelevant.

Apparent failure = Fix.

---

### Fix Examples

• “My test results were never shared.”
• “No one followed up after my abnormal report.”
• “The appointment was cancelled without informing me.”
• “I was discharged without instructions.”
• “The system showed my appointment confirmed, but the clinic said I wasn’t scheduled.”
• “I reported worsening symptoms and no one responded.”

---

### Fix Boundary

• ✅ Experience exists  
• ❌ Minimum standards of correctness, safety, communication, or responsibility not met  

Fix is **non-negotiable**.

---

## 🛠️ 2️⃣ OPTIMIZE — Reduce Avoidable Patient Effort

### Definition

Assign **Optimize** only when:

• the experience **did occur**
• the expected outcome **was ultimately delivered**
• no safety, correctness, or trust breach occurred
• but the journey involved avoidable effort, delay, repetition, or confusion

Optimize addresses **efficiency**, not failure.

---

### Optimize Examples

• “I got my appointment, but had to call multiple times.”
• “The instructions were confusing, but everything went fine.”
• “The check-in process worked, but took too long.”
• “I received my results, but had to keep refreshing the portal.”
• “Too many forms asked for the same information.”

---

### Optimize Boundary

• ✅ Exists  
• ✅ Works  
• ❌ Friction remains  

Never assign Optimize if:
• responsibility was unmet
• response was missing
• trust or reliability was broken

That is Fix.

---

## 🧪 3️⃣ INNOVATE — Create What Does Not Exist

### Definition

Assign **Innovate** only when the patient is requesting a
**net-new capability** that does not currently exist.

Innovate is about **creation**, not improvement or repair.

---

### Innovate Examples

• “Offer virtual physiotherapy after surgery.”
• “Allow same-day digital delivery of lab results.”
• “Provide remote monitoring for chronic patients.”

---

### Innovate Boundary

• ❌ Capability does not exist  
• ✅ Net-new build required  

Never assign Innovate if:
• something already exists but works poorly → Fix
• something exists but is inefficient → Optimize

---

### Innovation Sub-Tier (Innovate Only)

| Tier | Label                  | Meaning |
|-----:|------------------------|---------|
| 1    | Hygiene Expectation    | Baseline expectation |
| 2    | Convenience Enhancer  | Reduces effort |
| 3    | Experience Upgrade    | Improves reassurance or clarity |
| 4    | New Capability        | Adds meaningful clinical utility |
| 5    | Transformational Shift| Redefines care delivery |

---

## 📣 4️⃣ AMPLIFY — Scale What Builds Trust and Loyalty

### Definition

Assign **Amplify** only when the experience:

• exists  
• works exceptionally well  
• is explicitly praised  
• creates trust, reassurance, or emotional uplift  

Amplify protects and scales strengths.

---

### Amplify Examples

• “The nurse followed up personally the same day.”
• “The doctor explained everything clearly and calmly.”
• “The telehealth visit was seamless and reassuring.”

---

### Amplify Boundary

• ✅ Exists  
• ✅ Works exceptionally well  
• ❌ No complaint, request, or missing capability present  

If any issue exists → do not use Amplify.

---

## 🔎 OMS Diagnostic Flow

Does the experience exist?
├─ No → INNOVATE
│
├─ Yes → Did it fail, break, or violate responsibility?
│        ├─ Yes → FIX
│        └─ No → Did it cause avoidable effort or confusion?
│               ├─ Yes → OPTIMIZE
│               └─ No → Was it explicitly praised?
│                      ├─ Yes → AMPLIFY
│                      └─ No → Exclude / Unclear

---

## 🧠 Fix vs Optimize — Hard Determination Rule (Healthcare)

**Fix vs Optimize is determined solely by the patient’s stated experience
of failure or unmet responsibility — not by perceived severity,
clinical importance, or institutional judgment.**

### Use FIX when the patient explicitly indicates:
• failure, incorrectness, or non-occurrence  
• non-response or omission  
• breach of expected duty of care  

### Use OPTIMIZE only when the patient explicitly indicates:
• outcome achieved  
• no safety or trust breach  
• friction without failure  

---

## 🚫 Explicit Guardrails

• Do not upgrade to Fix based on:
  – inferred clinical risk
  – regulatory sensitivity
  – organizational fear

• Do not downgrade to Optimize if:
  – responsibility was missed
  – response did not occur
  – trust or reliability was broken

---

## 🔒 Lock-in Statement

Fix and Optimize are separated by
**patient-declared failure vs patient-declared friction**.

If the experience failed or responsibility was unmet → **Fix**  
If the experience worked but required excess effort → **Optimize**

This rule is invariant across Healthcare, FinTech, Insurance, and beyond.

🧠 SECTION 8: Experience Driver – Customer Effort Score (CES)
(Healthcare Edition — DecipherOS)

📌 Purpose

Customer Effort Score (CES) captures the **experienced effort** required by the
patient to engage with a **specific Experience Driver**.

CES is always:
• driver-specific (never review-level)
• localized to the interaction surface
• reflective of perceived effort, not outcome quality

CES does NOT:
• determine Opportunity Maximisation Stream
• imply correctness, safety, or success
• override Feedback Type or OMS

A high-effort experience can exist in Fix, Optimize, or Innovate.
CES measures **how hard it was**, not **what should be done**.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• One Experience Driver → one CES
• CES reflects **patient-experienced friction or fluency**
• CES is derived deterministically from Emotion + OMS + Context
• CES must never be inflated by inferred risk or institutional concern
• If effort is unclear, default to the lowest defensible score within bounds

---

## 📊 CES Scale (1–7)

CES uses a fixed 7-point scale:

1 → Effortless, seamless, intuitive  
2 → Very low effort  
3 → Low effort, minor friction  
4 → Moderate effort, noticeable friction  
5 → High effort, delays or repetition  
6 → Very high effort, major frustration  
7 → Extreme effort, task effectively blocked  

---

## 🔍 CES Derivation Framework

CES is derived in **three deterministic passes**.

---

### 1️⃣ Emotion → Initial Effort Band

Emotion provides the **starting perception of effort**.

| Emotion Group | Default Effort Perception | Initial CES Band |
|---------------|---------------------------|------------------|
| Adoration     | Effortless                | 1                |
| Appreciation  | Low effort                | 2–3              |
| Ambivalence   | Moderate effort           | 4                |
| Agitation     | High effort               | 5–6              |
| Anger         | Extreme effort            | 7                |

Emotion sets the **initial band only**.
It does not finalize the score.

---

### 2️⃣ OMS → Hard Constraint Layer

Opportunity Maximisation Stream constrains CES to ensure internal consistency.

| OMS      | Allowed CES Values |
|----------|--------------------|
| Amplify  | 1–3                |
| Optimize | 3–4                |
| Innovate | 4                  |
| Fix      | 5–7                |

Hard constraints:

• Amplify → CES must be 1, 2, or 3  
• Optimize → CES must be 3 or 4  
• Innovate → CES must be exactly 4  
• Fix → CES must be 5, 6, or 7  

OMS constrains CES **numerically only**.
It does not imply severity, urgency, or blame.

---

### 3️⃣ Contextual Refinement (Within Bounds)

Within the OMS-constrained range, refine CES using **contextual evidence only**.

Increase CES if the patient describes:
• repeated attempts or retries
• prolonged delays or waiting
• confusion requiring extra effort
• blockage of a critical journey step

Decrease CES if the patient indicates:
• task completion despite friction
• availability of a workaround
• mild inconvenience without disruption

---

## 🛑 Override Conditions (Explicit)

Set CES to:

• **7** → Task could not be completed; no workaround existed  
• **6** → Task completed with severe frustration or breakdown risk  
• **5** → Task completed but inefficient, stressful, or disruptive  

Overrides must be justified by **explicit patient language**.

Always return CES as a quoted string:
