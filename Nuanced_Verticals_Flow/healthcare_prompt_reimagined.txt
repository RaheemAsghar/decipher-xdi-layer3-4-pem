## 🧠 SECTION 3: Experience Driver – Specific Context **(Healthcare Edition)**

---

### 📌 Purpose

The **Experience Driver Context** explains **why a specific Experience Driver exists**.

It isolates the **exact patient-perceived trigger, failure, friction, success, or opportunity** that caused *this* Experience Driver to 
surface — **independent of all other signals** in the same feedback record.

Context is **not**:

• narrative
• summary
• interpretation
• intent
• emotion

Context is the **behavioral, responsibility-bound explanation** that makes the Experience Driver:

• auditable
• actionable
• and executable

Each Experience Driver must be contextualized **in isolation**, even when multiple drivers originate from the same interaction.

This context is a **first-class execution input** for:

• ACTION_AXIS routing
• risk and impact assessment
• Orchestration Unit (OU) formation
• PDCA planning and outcome evaluation

⚠️ Context must bind **exclusively** to the specific Experience Driver under evaluation.
Never generalize across the full feedback record.

---

### 🧭 Context Principles (Locked)

• Context must describe a **patient-impacting moment**
• Context must reflect an **apparent failure, friction, success, or opportunity**
• Context must remain **within the organization’s domain of responsibility**
• Context must be **independently actionable**
• Context must be **execution-neutral** (no intent or emotion leakage)

---

## 📍 Context Extraction Rules

---

### 1️⃣ Responsibility-Bound Specificity

Extract context only when the feedback **explicitly links** the Experience Driver to a concrete patient-impacting event, delay, defect, incident, or success.

Context must answer **what happened**, **where it manifested**, or
**what obligation was unmet or fulfilled** — nothing more.

🟢 Valid Context:
• “Appointment was cancelled on the same day without notification.”
• “Lab results were uploaded two days late, delaying treatment.”
• “Telehealth session disconnected twice during consultation.”

🔴 Invalid Context:
• “The hospital is fine.”
• “I like the clinic building.”
• “Healthcare is slow.”

---

### 2️⃣ Keyword Anchoring (Evidence Binding)

Extract keywords that anchor the context to a **real operational, clinical, or experiential surface**.

Keywords must expose **what failed, worked, or constrained execution**.

Keywords must **not** express emotion or opinion.

| Context                                      | Keywords                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| “MRI results were misplaced; scan repeated.” | Lost records, duplicate scan, admin error |
| “Doctor explained diagnosis clearly.”        | Patient education, explanation quality    |
| “Booking system crashed during scheduling.”  | System outage, booking failure            |

Keywords exist to reinforce **investigability**, not sentiment.

---

### 3️⃣ Actionable Depth Requirement

Context must contain sufficient clarity to support **one of the four ACTION_AXIS responses** without naming it explicitly.

Pure emotional expression without a causal anchor is invalid.

🟢 Include:
• “Medication was out of stock and no alternative was offered.”
• “Insurance claim denied without explanation.”

🔴 Exclude:
• “Hospitals are frustrating.”
• “This clinic is not for me.”

---

### 4️⃣ Multi-Outcome Context Handling

If a **single Experience Driver** produces **multiple causally independent outcomes**, each outcome must be captured as a **separate contextual instance**.

Do **not** split context for:
• emotional variation
• tone changes
• restatement of the same issue

🧩 Example:

Feedback:
“The doctor was very attentive, but the billing process took over an hour.”

Extract:
• Context A (Clinical Interaction): “Doctor explained treatment clearly.”
• Context B (Billing Process): “Billing process caused excessive delay.”

---

### 5️⃣ Business-Critical Priority Override

Always extract context that surfaces:

• patient safety risks or adverse events
• clinical or administrative errors
• consent, privacy, or regulatory breaches
• access barriers affecting care delivery

These contexts override precision thresholds and must be captured even if language is imprecise or emotionally neutral.

🟢 Mandatory:
• “Wrong patient file shown during consultation.”
• “Medical records shared without consent.”
• “Clinic not wheelchair accessible.”

---

### 6️⃣ Non-Actionable Context Exclusion

If an Experience Driver is valid but the context adds **no causal or executional information**, discard the instance.

🟢 Valid:
• “Surgery date was changed without informing me.”
• “Discharge summary listed incorrect medication dosage.”

🔴 Invalid:
• “The hospital is decent.”
• “No issues so far.”

---

## 📦 Required Context Payload (Per Driver)

Each Experience Driver instance must include:

• Experience Driver — canonical Category → Subcategory
• Context — concise, responsibility-bound explanation
• Keywords — operational anchors tied to the context

Context **enriches execution**.
It **never alters identity, intent, or affect**.

---

### 🧠 Anti-Ambiguity Enforcement

• Do not infer causes not stated
• Do not normalize or soften failures
• Do not generalize across episodes
• Do not invent precision

If timing, interaction moment, or process step is unclear:
→ record **“Unspecified”**

---

### 🧠 Enforcement Statement

This layer is an **execution integrity boundary**.

No Orchestration Unit, PDCA plan, or outcome evaluation may be generated from an Experience Driver lacking clear, responsibility-bound context.

Context exists to turn Experience Drivers into **executable truth** — not story.

# 🧠 SECTION 4: Experience Driver – INTENT AXIS **(Healthcare Edition)**

---

## 📌 Purpose

The **INTENT AXIS** classifies the **communicative intent** of a specific Experience Driver in healthcare.

It answers **one question only**:

> **What class of organizational obligation or response is the patient invoking through this signal?**

INTENT AXIS is applied **per Experience Driver**, never per review.

It does **not**:
• measure emotion
• describe severity
• assign priority
• determine effort or urgency

It determines **how the healthcare system is permitted to respond**.

---

## 🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• One Experience Driver → **one INTENT classification**
• INTENT reflects **what the patient expects the organization to do**
• Classification is based on **patient-invoked obligation**, not wording
• Clinical safety, access, and financial risk **always dominate**
• Ambiguity must resolve toward **duty of care**, never generosity

If multiple intents exist, the signal **must be split** at the Experience Driver level.

---

## 🔐 Canonical INTENT AXIS SET (Healthcare — Closed)

Each Experience Driver must be classified into **exactly one** of the following seven Healthcare-specific INTENT categories.

No additions.
No hybrids.
No re-labeling.

---

### 1️⃣ **Clinical Praise**

*(Trust reinforcement signal)*

#### ✅ Definition

Explicit positive reinforcement for a **specific clinical, care, or service interaction** that **built trust**, increased confidence, or demonstrated
professional excellence.

Clinical Praise identifies **what must be protected and scaled**.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “The doctor explained my diagnosis clearly and patiently.”
• “The nurse made sure I understood my discharge instructions.”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Generic approval without specificity (“Great hospital”)
• Praise without a patient-visible determinant

---

### 2️⃣ **Safety & Financial Risk Concern**

*(Duty-of-care breach or perceived harm)*

#### ✅ Definition

Any signal that surfaces **actual or perceived risk** involving:

• patient safety or clinical harm
• medication, diagnosis, or treatment error
• billing shock, insurance denial, or financial exposure
• access barriers affecting timely care
• rights, consent, or privacy violations

This INTENT always represents a **non-negotiable organizational obligation**.

It **always routes to Restore Safety or Fix-class execution** downstream.

#### 🛑 Mandatory Classification

Always classify as **Safety & Financial Risk Concern** when feedback involves:

• unsafe conditions or care errors
• missed or delayed clinical follow-up
• incorrect billing or insurance handling
• access denial with care impact
• privacy or consent breaches

Tone, politeness, and emotion are irrelevant.

#### 🟢 Examples:

• “I was charged twice for the same procedure.”
• “My test results were delayed and no one followed up.”
• “I was given the wrong dosage instructions.”

---

### 3️⃣ **Care Coordination Request**

*(Expectation of enablement or continuity)*

#### ✅ Definition

An explicit expectation for **coordination, access, or enablement** required
to continue or manage care.

These are **operational obligations**, not optional enhancements.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “Please send my records to the specialist.”
• “I need to schedule a follow-up appointment.”
• “Can someone call me with my test results?”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Requests for brand-new, non-core capabilities → Innovate downstream
• Optional comfort enhancements → Process Improvement Suggestion

---

### 4️⃣ **Process Improvement Suggestion**

*(Optional operational enhancement)*

#### ✅ Definition

A **non-critical**, non-obligatory suggestion that would improve comfort, convenience, or efficiency **without affecting safety, access, or correctness**.

Suggestions never imply failure or unmet duty.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “It would help to have clearer signage.”
• “Text reminders could reduce missed appointments.”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Anything implying obligation, urgency, or risk
• Anything tied to access, billing, or care continuity

---

### 5️⃣ **Clinical Clarification Question**

*(Information or understanding gap)*

#### ✅ Definition

A genuine request for explanation or understanding related to:

• diagnosis
• treatment
• test results
• next steps
• care policies

Questions do **not** assert failure unless explicitly stated.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “What does this lab value mean?”
• “How long should I expect side effects?”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Rhetorical accusations framed as questions
(“Why does no one ever explain anything?”)

---

### 6️⃣ **Patient Behavior Pattern**

*(Observed usage or adherence behavior)*

#### ✅ Definition

Description of **actual patient behavior** in interacting with care, services, or systems — including workarounds, avoidance, or habitual usage.

These signals surface **latent friction or design opportunity**.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “I only book telehealth for urgent issues.”
• “I skip follow-ups because scheduling is difficult.”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Opinions without behavioral evidence

---

### 7️⃣ **Healthcare Trend Signal**

*(External benchmark or expectation shift)*

#### ✅ Definition

A comparative or forward-looking signal referencing **external standards, industry evolution, or peer healthcare practices**.

These signals inform **strategic evolution**, not immediate correction.

#### 🟢 Include:

• “Other hospitals offer same-day lab results.”
• “Most clinics provide online access to imaging.”

#### 🔴 Exclude:

• Internal complaints without comparison

---

## 🔍 INTENT AXIS Enforcement Rules

• Exactly **one INTENT per Experience Driver**
• Split compound signals
• Safety & Financial Risk Concern **always dominates**
• If unclear between Process Improvement vs Care Coordination → Care Coordination
• If unclear between Clarification vs Risk → Safety & Financial Risk Concern

---

## 📦 Required Output Fields (Per Driver)

Each Experience Driver instance must include:

• Experience Driver
• **INTENT AXIS classification**
• Context (verbatim excerpt tied to that driver)

---

## 🧠 Enforcement Statement

The INTENT AXIS is a **response-governance boundary**.

Misclassification corrupts:

• ACTION AXIS routing
• Restore Safety vs Improve Care separation
• Orchestration Unit legitimacy
• Downstream PDCA integrity

No Experience Driver may proceed downstream without a **single, defensible Healthcare INTENT classification**.

🧠 SECTION 5: Experience Driver – AFFECT AXIS **(Healthcare Edition)**

📌 Purpose

The AFFECT AXIS captures the dominant emotional pressure associated with each extracted Experience Driver, as expressed by the patient or caregiver.

In Healthcare, affect reflects perceived levels of:
• trust
• reassurance
• uncertainty
• anxiety
• or fear

Affect must be bound to the specific Experience Driver, not to the overall patient comment.

Affect functions as a weighting and interpretation signal for downstream deterministic computation (e.g., ERI, prioritization posture, escalation tone).

Affect does NOT:
• determine Feedback Type (Intent Axis)
• determine the Opportunity Stream (Action Axis)
• change Experience Driver identity
• imply clinical diagnosis, severity, or medical risk

Feedback Type governs response class.
Affect governs emotional pressure.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• Affect is assigned per Experience Driver, never per review
• One affect tier per driver (most behaviorally meaningful)
• Affect must be grounded in patient language, tone, and context
• No imaginative inference, no clinical interpretation, no escalation
• If affect is unclear or emotionally flat → default to Uncertainty (0) with low confidence

⚠️ Boundary Reminder:
This layer captures patient-expressed emotional reality, not medical, clinical, or institutional judgment.

---

🔐 Controlled AFFECT Framework (Healthcare — Authoritative)

All affect classification must use the following **five-tier Healthcare AFFECT AXIS**, which replaces all universal or generic emotion models for this vertical.

| Tier | Affect Label   | Meaning (Locked) |
|-----:|---------------|------------------|
| +3   | Trust         | Confidence in care, diagnosis, provider, and outcomes |
| +1   | Reassurance   | Feeling cared for, informed, emotionally settled |
| 0    | Uncertainty   | Awaiting clarity, results, decisions, or next steps |
| −1   | Anxiety       | Worry or confusion, including administrative and financial stress (billing shock, insurance confusion, access barriers) |
| −3   | Fear          | Perceived or real safety risk, harm, or medical danger |

No other affect labels are permitted.

This framework is:
• vertical-specific
• polarity-aware
• execution-stable

---

🔎 AFFECT Assignment Logic (Deterministic)

1️⃣ Context-First  
Determine affect from the lived experience described — delays, risk exposure,
confusion, reassurance, safety concerns, or trust reinforcement — not from
keyword spotting.

2️⃣ Driver-Bound  
The affect must apply to the specific Experience Driver being emitted.

3️⃣ Pressure Calibration  
Select the tier that best reflects the level of emotional pressure carried by
the experience, without exaggeration or minimization.

4️⃣ Single Assignment Rule  
Exactly one AFFECT tier per Experience Driver.

5️⃣ Uncertainty Handling  
If affect cannot be confidently determined:
• Affect: Uncertainty
• Tier: 0
• Confidence: Low

6️⃣ Sarcasm Handling  
Classify affect by underlying meaning, not surface wording.
Example:
“Great, another cancelled appointment.” → Anxiety or Fear (depending on risk context)

---

🧷 Optional Specific Feeling (Non-Governing)

A short descriptive feeling (e.g., “Frustrated”, “Relieved”, “Worried”) may be included only if it clarifies the selected AFFECT tier.

Specific feelings:
• are optional
• do not change the tier
• have no computational authority
• exist only to aid human interpretability

---

📦 Output Format (Strict)

Affect: [Trust | Reassurance | Uncertainty | Anxiety | Fear]  
Confidence: [High | Medium | Low]

Example:
Affect: Anxiety  
Confidence: Medium

---

⚙️ Interpretive Posture (Non-Governing)

Affect influences response tone and urgency framing only:

• Trust        → reinforcement, continuity signals
• Reassurance  → stabilization and clarity reinforcement
• Uncertainty  → activation, explanation, or follow-up
• Anxiety      → friction resolution and reassurance posture
• Fear         → escalation, safety restoration, trust recovery

Affect never alters execution class.

---

🧠 Enforcement Statement

The AFFECT AXIS is a vertical execution primitive.

Any attempt to:
• reintroduce universal emotion groups
• infer clinical severity from affect
• override Intent or Action axes using affect

is a hard architectural violation.

This system encodes emotional pressure for execution — not sentiment analysis.

This classification ensures that orchestration targets the **precise behavioral trigger** that caused the Experience Driver to exist — not a generalized or

This layer is mandatory for:
• execution-grade OU targeting  
• PDCA precision  
• root-cause correlation  
• behavioral traceability  

---

🧭 Core Principle (Locked)

Journey classification is a **behavioral timestamp**, not a taxonomy label.

It answers:
• When did the experience occur?
• Where in the care flow did it manifest?
• What exact interaction triggered the patient’s response?

Classification must be bound to the **specific Experience Driver**, never to the overall feedback record.

---

## 🔍 Required Output (Per Experience Driver)

For each Experience Driver, you must return **all three fields**:

### 1️⃣ Patient Journey
The broad phase of the healthcare experience in which the interaction occurred.

## 🧠 SECTION 7: Experience Driver – Action Axis (OMS) **(Healthcare Edition)**

### 📌 Purpose

For each extracted Experience Driver, assign **one and only one** **Healthcare Action Axis (OMS)**:

1. **Restore Safety**
2. **Improve Care Delivery**
3. **Scale Clinical Excellence**
4. **Innovate Care Model**

This Action Axis defines the **single authoritative execution pathway** the system is permitted to take in response to the Experience Driver.

OMS is:

* driver-specific (never review-level)
* mutually exclusive
* grounded in the patient’s stated experience
* enforced before OU formation and PDCA planning

OMS selection determines **what kind of action is allowed**, not how it is executed.

---

### 🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

* One Experience Driver → one OMS
* OMS is determined by **patient-declared reality**, not inferred risk
* Feedback Type constrains OMS eligibility
* Emotion does not determine OMS
* Apparent failure always dominates friction

If multiple interpretations seem possible, resolve toward
**organizational obligation**, not optimism.

---

## 🔍 OMS Determination Inputs (Mandatory)

OMS must be determined using only:

* Experience Driver (identity)
* Context (what happened)
* Feedback Type (intent class)
* Interaction Moment (trigger window)
* Explicit patient language

You must NOT use:

* inferred clinical severity
* regulatory sensitivity
* institutional anxiety
* hypothetical downstream harm

---

## 🛠️ Canonical OMS Set (Closed)

Exactly one stream must be selected.

| OMS (Healthcare Action Axis)  | Core Meaning                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Restore Safety**            | Exists but failed, broke, was unsafe, or unmet responsibility |
| **Improve Care Delivery**     | Exists and worked, but caused avoidable effort or friction    |
| **Scale Clinical Excellence** | Exists and worked exceptionally well                          |
| **Innovate Care Model**       | Does not exist; patient desires it to exist                   |

---

## 🛠️ 1️⃣ RESTORE SAFETY — Resolve Apparent Failure or Unmet Responsibility

### Definition

Assign **Restore Safety** when the experience **existed** but:

* failed to occur as expected
* was incorrect, unsafe, unreliable, or incomplete
* involved non-response or omission
* breached reasonable patient expectations of duty of care

Restore Safety applies **regardless of root cause, intent, or attribution**.

If the patient indicates that something **should have happened and did not**, the correct stream is **Restore Safety**.

---

### Mandatory Restore Safety Triggers

Always assign Restore Safety when feedback includes:

* missed, delayed, or incorrect care delivery
* non-response to patient outreach or alerts
* appointment failures or unexplained cancellations
* test results lost, delayed, or not communicated
* medication errors or unclear instructions
* diagnostic or treatment delays
* patient safety concerns or adverse incidents
* administrative or clinical process breakdowns

Tone is irrelevant.
Severity is irrelevant.
Politeness is irrelevant.

Apparent failure = Restore Safety.

---

### Restore Safety Examples

* “My test results were never shared.”
* “No one followed up after my abnormal report.”
* “The appointment was cancelled without informing me.”
* “I was discharged without instructions.”
* “The system showed my appointment confirmed, but the clinic said I wasn’t scheduled.”
* “I reported worsening symptoms and no one responded.”

---

### Restore Safety Boundary

* ✅ Experience exists
* ❌ Minimum standards of correctness, safety, communication, or responsibility not met

Restore Safety is **non-negotiable**.

---

## ⚙️ 2️⃣ IMPROVE CARE DELIVERY — Reduce Avoidable Patient Effort

### Definition

Assign **Improve Care Delivery** only when:

* the experience **did occur**
* the expected outcome **was ultimately delivered**
* no safety, correctness, or trust breach occurred
* but the journey involved avoidable effort, delay, repetition, or confusion

Improve Care Delivery addresses **efficiency**, not failure.

---

### Improve Care Delivery Examples

* “I got my appointment, but had to call multiple times.”
* “The instructions were confusing, but everything went fine.”
* “The check-in process worked, but took too long.”
* “I received my results, but had to keep refreshing the portal.”
* “Too many forms asked for the same information.”

---

### Improve Care Delivery Boundary

* ✅ Exists
* ✅ Works
* ❌ Friction remains

Never assign Improve Care Delivery if:

* responsibility was unmet
* response was missing
* trust or reliability was broken

That is **Restore Safety**.

---

## 🧪 3️⃣ INNOVATE CARE MODEL — Create What Does Not Exist

### Definition

Assign **Innovate Care Model** only when the patient is requesting a **net-new capability** that does not currently exist.

Innovate Care Model is about **creation**, not improvement or repair.

---

### Innovate Care Model Examples

* “Offer virtual physiotherapy after surgery.”
* “Allow same-day digital delivery of lab results.”
* “Provide remote monitoring for chronic patients.”

---

### Innovate Care Model Boundary

* ❌ Capability does not exist
* ✅ Net-new build required

Never assign Innovate Care Model if:

* something already exists but works poorly → Restore Safety
* something exists but is inefficient → Improve Care Delivery

---

### Innovation Sub-Tier (Innovate Care Model Only)

| Tier | Label                  | Meaning                          |
| ---: | ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
|    1 | Hygiene Expectation    | Baseline expectation             |
|    2 | Convenience Enhancer   | Reduces effort                   |
|    3 | Experience Upgrade     | Improves reassurance or clarity  |
|    4 | New Capability         | Adds meaningful clinical utility |
|    5 | Transformational Shift | Redefines care delivery          |

---

## 📣 4️⃣ SCALE CLINICAL EXCELLENCE — Scale What Builds Trust and Loyalty

### Definition

Assign **Scale Clinical Excellence** only when the experience:

* exists
* works exceptionally well
* is explicitly praised
* creates trust, reassurance, or emotional uplift

Scale Clinical Excellence protects and scales strengths.

---

### Scale Clinical Excellence Examples

* “The nurse followed up personally the same day.”
* “The doctor explained everything clearly and calmly.”
* “The telehealth visit was seamless and reassuring.”

---

### Scale Clinical Excellence Boundary

* ✅ Exists
* ✅ Works exceptionally well
* ❌ No complaint, request, or missing capability present

If any issue exists → do not use Scale Clinical Excellence.

---

## 🔎 OMS Diagnostic Flow

Does the experience exist?
├─ No → **INNOVATE CARE MODEL**
│
├─ Yes → Did it fail, break, or violate responsibility?
│        ├─ Yes → **RESTORE SAFETY**
│        └─ No → Did it cause avoidable effort or confusion?
│               ├─ Yes → **IMPROVE CARE DELIVERY**
│               └─ No → Was it explicitly praised?
│                      ├─ Yes → **SCALE CLINICAL EXCELLENCE**
│                      └─ No → Exclude / Unclear

## 🧠 Restore Safety vs Improve Care Delivery — Hard Determination Rule (Healthcare)

**Restore Safety vs Improve Care Delivery is determined solely by the patient’s stated experience of failure or unmet responsibility — not by 
perceived severity, clinical importance, or institutional judgment.**

### Use RESTORE SAFETY when the patient explicitly indicates:

* failure, incorrectness, or non-occurrence
* non-response or omission
* breach of expected duty of care

### Use IMPROVE CARE DELIVERY only when the patient explicitly indicates:

* outcome achieved
* no safety or trust breach
* friction without failure

---

## 🚫 Explicit Guardrails

* Do not upgrade to Restore Safety based on:
  – inferred clinical risk
  – regulatory sensitivity
  – organizational fear

* Do not downgrade to Improve Care Delivery if:
  – responsibility was missed
  – response did not occur
  – trust or reliability was broken

---

## 🔒 Lock-in Statement

Restore Safety and Improve Care Delivery are separated by **patient-declared failure vs patient-declared friction** (or unmet responsibility).

If the experience failed or responsibility was unmet → **Restore Safety**
If the experience worked but required excess effort → **Improve Care Delivery**

This rule is invariant across Healthcare, FinTech, Insurance, and beyond.

🧠 SECTION 8: Experience Driver – Customer Effort Score **(Healthcare Edition)**      

📌 Purpose

Customer Effort Score (CES) captures the **experienced effort** required by the patient to engage with a **specific Experience Driver**.

CES is always:
• driver-specific (never review-level)
• localized to the interaction surface
• reflective of perceived effort, not outcome quality

CES does NOT:
• determine Opportunity Maximisation Stream
• imply correctness, safety, or success
• override Feedback Type or OMS

A high-effort experience can exist in Fix, Optimize, or Innovate.
CES measures **how hard it was**, not **what should be done**.

---

🧭 Core Principles (Locked)

• One Experience Driver → one CES
• CES reflects **patient-experienced friction or fluency**
• CES is derived deterministically from Emotion + OMS + Context
• CES must never be inflated by inferred risk or institutional concern
• If effort is unclear, default to the lowest defensible score within bounds

---

## 📊 CES Scale (1–7)

CES uses a fixed 7-point scale:

1 → Effortless, seamless, intuitive  
2 → Very low effort  
3 → Low effort, minor friction  
4 → Moderate effort, noticeable friction  
5 → High effort, delays or repetition  
6 → Very high effort, major frustration  
7 → Extreme effort, task effectively blocked  

---

## 🔍 CES Derivation Framework

CES is derived in **three deterministic passes**.

---

### 1️⃣ Emotion → Initial Effort Band

Emotion provides the **starting perception of effort**.

| Healthcare Affect | Meaning (recap)                      | Initial CES Band |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| **Trust**         | Confidence in care & outcomes        | 1–2              |
| **Reassurance**   | Felt cared for, informed             | 2–3              |
| **Uncertainty**   | Awaiting clarity                     | 4                |
| **Anxiety**       | Worry incl. admin / financial stress | 5–6              |
| **Fear**          | Safety risk / harm                   | 6–7              |


Emotion sets the **initial band only**.
It does not finalize the score.

---

### 2️⃣ OMS → Hard Constraint Layer

Opportunity Maximisation Stream constrains CES to ensure internal consistency.

| Healthcare OMS                | Allowed CES Values |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Scale Clinical Excellence** | 1–3                |
| **Improve Care Delivery**     | 3–4                |
| **Innovate Care Model**       | 4                  |
| **Restore Safety**            | 5–7                |

**Hard constraints:**

• **Scale Clinical Excellence** → CES must be **1, 2, or 3**
• **Improve Care Delivery** → CES must be **3 or 4**
• **Innovate Care Model** → CES must be **exactly 4**
• **Restore Safety** → CES must be **5, 6, or 7**

### 3️⃣ Contextual Refinement (Within Bounds)

Within the OMS-constrained range, refine CES using **contextual evidence only**.

Increase CES if the patient describes:
• repeated attempts or retries
• prolonged delays or waiting
• confusion requiring extra effort
• blockage of a critical journey step

Decrease CES if the patient indicates:
• task completion despite friction
• availability of a workaround
• mild inconvenience without disruption

---

## 🛑 Override Conditions (Explicit)

Set CES to:

• **7** → Task could not be completed; no workaround existed  
• **6** → Task completed with severe frustration or breakdown risk  
• **5** → Task completed but inefficient, stressful, or disruptive  

Overrides must be justified by **explicit patient language**.

## 📦 Output Format (Strict)

Always return CES as a quoted string:

```json
"Customer Effort Score": "6"
```
