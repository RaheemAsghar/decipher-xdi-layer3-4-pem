problem_statement_prompt = """

# 🎯 DECIPHER PDCA – PROBLEM STATEMENT GENERATION PROMPT 

## 🔬 **Purpose**
Generate a **diagnostic-quality, emotionally faithful problem statement** to kick off the Plan phase 
of the PDCA lifecycle.
This is a **semantic paragraph**, not a stitched block. It must combine emotional truth, 
contextual grounding, strategic framing, and executional urgency.

---

## 🔐 **You Are Given**

A fully structured Orchestration Unit (OU) with the following canonical inputs:

* `semantic_action_statement` → A two-part narrative with Section 1 (Customer Reality) and Section 2 (Strategic Response)
* `customer_journey` → e.g., “Document Management”
* `journey_stage` → e.g., “Verification Rejection”
* `stream_justification` → Reason why Fix / Optimize / Amplify / Innovate was chosen
* `behavioral_impact` → Consequence of ignoring or missing this moment

---

## 🧬 **Construction Framework**

| Component            | Pull From |
|----------------------|-----------|
| **WHAT** → What happened? Combine emotional narrative and behavioral friction | `semantic_action_statement` + `behavioral_impact` |
| **WHEN / WHERE** → Journey context | `customer_journey`, `journey_stage` |
| **WHY** → Why this stream is needed (structural or strategic reasoning) | `stream_justification` |
| **SO WHAT** → What risk is at stake if unresolved | `behavioral_impact` |

---

🧩 Tactic: Let semantic_action_statement guide the emotion and trigger, but use behavioral_impact to 
enhance the problem definition beyond the user's experience — into platform-level or strategic harm.

---

## ✅ **Output Format**

problem_statement: >
  [One fluent paragraph (2–5 sentences) combining all elements above]


---

## ❌ What NOT to Do
* Don’t stitch raw fields
* Don’t regurgitate semantic\_action\_1 and 2 separately
* Don’t ignore emotional tone
* Don’t fabricate stream logic — it's already provided
* Don’t write generic consequences — use the provided behavioral impact

---

✅ Examples

### Example: 1

🎛️ Inputs:
semantic_action_statement: >
  SECTION 1: THE CUSTOMER REALITY
  The customer submitted a Complaint about receiving duplicate debit notifications for a single credit card payment. Although one charge was reversed, they experienced intense confusion and fear that their account had been compromised.

  SECTION 2: THE STRATEGIC RESPONSE
  We need to Fix → Transaction Processing → Duplicate Charge Management to eliminate volatile transaction errors that destabilize user trust and expose them to unnecessary emotional and financial stress.

customer_journey: "Transaction Experience"
journey_stage: "Payment Processing"
stream_justification: "Financial systems operate on a fragile trust curve. Even momentary anomalies like duplicate charges evoke panic, push users into defensive behaviors (freezing cards, calling support), and break digital confidence. Fixing this is mission-critical."
behavioral_impact: "Improve real-time payment event accuracy, introduce anomaly flags for duplicate detection, and embed immediate user-facing trust cues to reassure in-flight."

🧠 problem_statement:
problem_statement: >
  The customer submitted a Complaint about receiving duplicate debit notifications for a single credit card 
  payment, and although one charge was reversed, they experienced intense confusion and fear that their account 
  had been compromised. This occurred during Payment Processing within the Transaction Experience journey. 
  The orchestration stream is Fix — mission-critical resolution needed to restore financial system trust. It 
  was routed here because financial systems operate on a fragile trust curve where even momentary anomalies 
  like duplicate charges evoke panic, push users into defensive behaviors, and break digital confidence. If 
  unresolved, it risks continued payment accuracy issues, defensive user behaviors like freezing cards, and fundamental erosion of digital financial trust.

---

### Example: 2

🎛️ Inputs:
semantic_action_statement: >
  SECTION 1: THE CUSTOMER REALITY
  The customer made a Suggestion about wanting to dispute transactions directly in the app instead of being forced to call support.

  SECTION 2: THE STRATEGIC RESPONSE
  We need to Innovate → Dispute Management → In-App Resolution Options to enable frictionless, self-guided dispute initiation that aligns with modern expectations.

customer_journey: "Issue Resolution & Recovery"
journey_stage: "Transaction Dispute Path"
stream_justification: "This is a forward-looking request tied to UX modernity and emotional ease. The operational process exists but isn't accessible in the way customers want."
behavioral_impact: "Reduce support tickets, increase user control, and shift perception of the app as a self-reliant command center."

🧠 problem_statement:
problem_statement: >
  The customer made a Suggestion about wanting to dispute transactions directly in the app instead of being 
  forced to call support when trying to initiate a dispute via the Transaction Dispute Path within Issue 
  Resolution & Recovery. The orchestration stream is Innovate — designed to unlock new capabilities that enhance 
  user autonomy and align with modern expectations. It was routed here because this is a forward-looking request 
  tied to UX modernity and emotional ease, where the operational process exists but isn't accessible in the 
  way customers want. If not pursued, the business misses opportunities to reduce support tickets, increase 
  user control, and shift perception of the app as a self-reliant command center.

---

## 🔁 Final Note

> This is the **first sentence of the PDCA loop**.
> It must be emotionally clean, behaviorally precise, and strategically actionable.

---

"""
